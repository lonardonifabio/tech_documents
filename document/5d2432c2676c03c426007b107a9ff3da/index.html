Future of Work with AI Agents: Auditing Automation and Augmentation Potential across the U.S. Workforce

Authors: Yijia Shao, Humishka Zope, Yucheng Jiang, Jiaxin Pei, David Nguyen, Erik Brynjolfsson, Diyi Yang

This research paper investigates the impact of rapidly evolving compound AI systems (AI agents) on the U.S. workforce, specifically focusing on the potential for automation and augmentation. Recognizing a significant gap in understanding the evolving labor market landscape, the authors introduce a novel auditing framework designed to systematically assess worker preferences regarding AI agent involvement in various occupational tasks. The framework utilizes an audio-enhanced mini-interview, incorporating the Human Agency Scale (HAS) to quantify desired levels of human involvement. A key component of the research is the construction of the WORKBank database, leveraging the U.S. Department of Labor’s O*NET database as a foundation. This database incorporates data from 1,500 domain workers and capability assessments from AI experts, covering over 844 tasks across 104 occupations. The research categorizes these tasks into four distinct zones: an ‘Automation Green Light’ Zone indicating tasks suitable for automation, a ‘Automation Red Light’ Zone signifying tasks unsuitable for automation, an ‘R&D Opportunity Zone’ highlighting areas where AI augmentation presents research possibilities, and a ‘Low Priority Zone’ for tasks with limited potential. The study aims to identify critical mismatches between worker desires and the current technological capabilities of AI agents, providing insights for navigating the future of work in an age of increasingly sophisticated automation. The findings contribute to a more nuanced understanding of how AI agents can be effectively integrated into the workforce, mitigating potential negative consequences like job displacement and overreliance on automation, while also capitalizing on opportunities for innovation and productivity gains. The research methodology and resulting WORKBank database represent a valuable resource for researchers, policymakers, and businesses seeking to understand and prepare for the transformative effects of AI on the labor market.
