ARIA 0.1 Testing Scenarios and Protocols

Authors: NIST, NIST

This document details the testing procedures and scenarios employed in ARIA 0.1, a project focused on evaluating AI systems within a controlled environment. The core of the report centers around three distinct testing levels: model testing, red teaming, and field testing, each designed to rigorously assess the system's behavior and adherence to safety guidelines. ARIA 0.1 utilized three pilot scenarios – TV Spoilers, Meal Planner, and Pathfinder – to exercise metrology methods and explore both positive and negative impacts within pre-defined contexts. These scenarios were specifically crafted to simulate real-world applications and identify potential risks. The testing protocols were formally reviewed and approved by the NIST Research Protections Office (RPO) under protocols ITL - 2024 -0379, ITL -2024 -0384, and ITL -2024 -0376. Data from sessions utilizing other languages were excluded from the analysis to maintain the integrity of the report. A key concept introduced is the use of ‘proxies’ – applications designed to represent risks identified in NIST AI 600-1, allowing for broader risk assessment. Each scenario is accompanied by a ‘guardrail’ which defines the permissible and prohibited information that an application can share with a user, ensuring responsible AI development. The document provides descriptions for each pilot scenario, outlining the specific tasks and the associated guardrails that were enforced during evaluation. The TV Spoilers scenario, in particular, is described as a preliminary exploration. The overall goal of ARIA 0.1 is to establish a framework for evaluating AI systems, focusing on risk mitigation and responsible deployment. The document highlights the importance of controlled testing environments and the use of proxy applications to simulate real-world scenarios and identify potential vulnerabilities. The inclusion of NIST AI 700-2 November 2025 and the reference to NIST AI 600-1 further emphasizes the document's grounding in established AI risk management standards.
