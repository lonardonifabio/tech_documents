What 4,372 AI use cases reveal about moving beyond pilots

Authors: Elena Sügis, Grace Smith, Bobby Chandan, Andres Raieste

This report, authored by Nortal and its team of experts, investigates the current state of Artificial Intelligence (AI) adoption within the public sector, specifically focusing on government organizations. The research, based on an analysis of 4,372 AI use cases, reveals a critical issue: it’s not simply a lack of AI technology, but rather a misalignment between AI solutions and the specific needs of public sector entities. The study highlights that many AI projects fail to move beyond the Proof of Concept (PoC) phase, often due to a lack of strategic planning, insufficient citizen adoption, and challenges related to data governance, legal restrictions, and budget constraints. The report advocates for a sector-driven approach to AI capability building, emphasizing the importance of AI-augmented government services and AI-ready digital public infrastructure. It identifies key questions governments should ask when evaluating AI projects, including expectations regarding Return on Investment (ROI) and the potential for citizen engagement. Furthermore, the document explores the reasons behind project stalls, offering insights from successful AI deployments and outlining the complexities surrounding data challenges and legal considerations. The authors, including Elena Sügis, Grace Smith, Bobby Chandan, and Andres Raieste, draw upon their expertise at Nortal, a global innovation and technology company, to provide a practical guide for governments seeking to leverage AI effectively. The report’s central argument is that a shift from pilot-driven AI initiatives to strategically designed solutions aligned with public sector priorities is crucial for realizing the full potential of AI in government.
