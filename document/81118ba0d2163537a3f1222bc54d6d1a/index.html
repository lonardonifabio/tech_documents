Levels of Autonomy for AI Agents

Authors: K. J. Kevin Feng, David W. McDonald, Amy X. Zhang

This document, produced by the Knight 1st Amendment Institute at the University of Washington, explores the critical issue of autonomy levels for AI agents. It argues that agent autonomy should be a deliberate design choice, distinct from the agent's inherent capabilities and the operational environment. The authors propose a framework defining five escalating levels of agent autonomy: operator, collaborator, consultant, approver, and observer. Each level outlines the degree of user control and the nature of the user-agent interaction. Specifically, the 'operator' level involves direct control and execution, while the 'collaborator' level emphasizes joint planning and delegation. The 'consultant' level sees the agent taking the lead but seeking user expertise, and the 'approver' level involves the agent seeking user approval. Finally, the 'observer' level represents minimal user involvement. The document highlights the potential application of this framework for creating AI autonomy certificates, aiming to govern agent behavior in both single and multi-agent systems. The research addresses the responsible deployment of AI agents in the real world, offering practical steps for evaluating agent autonomy and designing effective user-agent interactions. The core argument centers on the need for a structured approach to managing the risks and opportunities presented by increasingly autonomous AI systems. The authors recognize that simply increasing an agent's capabilities doesn't automatically translate to beneficial outcomes; careful consideration of the appropriate level of control is paramount. The work contributes to a growing body of research focused on ensuring that AI agents are aligned with human values and goals, mitigating potential harms, and maximizing their utility. The framework presented provides a starting point for developers and researchers seeking to navigate the complex landscape of AI autonomy, offering a tangible methodology for assessing and managing the risks associated with increasingly sophisticated agents. Ultimately, the document advocates for a proactive and thoughtful approach to designing AI systems, prioritizing responsible innovation and the long-term benefits of human-AI collaboration.
