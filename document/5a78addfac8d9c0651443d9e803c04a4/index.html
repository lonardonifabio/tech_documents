AI Governance in Poland

Authors: Unknown

This document outlines Poland's approach to Artificial Intelligence (AI) governance, focusing on its alignment with the European Union's AI Act and the development of a comprehensive, adaptive model. The core strategy centers around leveraging data – including data lakes, data warehouses, and MLOps platforms – to drive innovation across various sectors, encompassing model training, automation, personalization, and process optimization. Key techniques like data augmentation, synthetic data generation, anonymization/pseudonymization, and federated learning are being actively implemented to address data challenges and ensure responsible AI development. The document highlights the crucial role of expanding broadband penetration and an open-data culture in facilitating large-scale, data-driven AI projects throughout the Polish economy. Poland's approach distinguishes itself through a pragmatic and adaptive strategy, moving beyond broad legislation to directly integrate with the EU AI Act. Specifically, the country is focusing on establishing national regulations concerning the notification of high-risk AI systems, defining the roles and responsibilities of supervisory bodies, and designating conformity assessment organizations. Furthermore, the Ministry of Digital Affairs and the Office for Personal Data Protection are collaboratively developing detailed guidelines for both the public and private sectors, ensuring compliance and promoting ethical AI practices. A central tenet of Poland's AI governance is prioritizing human rights, transparency, and practical application, aiming to create AI solutions that are not only innovative but also democratically grounded. The document emphasizes a globally harmonized ethics framework alongside EU-aligned oversight, suggesting a balanced approach to fostering AI innovation while mitigating potential risks. The focus on adaptation and direct integration with the EU AI Act demonstrates a commitment to staying at the forefront of AI regulation and ensuring Poland's participation in the evolving global landscape of AI governance. The document's scope extends to the operational aspects of AI, including the utilization of MLOps platforms and the implementation of best practices for model training and deployment.
