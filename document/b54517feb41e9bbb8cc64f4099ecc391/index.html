This document, titled "Serious Incident Reporting: Ensuring Public Safety," analyzes emerging trends in AI incident reporting, primarily focusing on data collected between May – September 2025. The core finding is that accountability and transparency are the most frequently reported areas within AI incidents, representing 336 reports in September 2025. Transparency, safety, and robustness also receive significant attention, though recent declines may indicate improvements in system resilience or changes in reporting criteria. Notably, concerns regarding fairness, human wellbeing, and sustainability consistently lag behind, suggesting potential gaps in risk assessment. The report emphasizes the need for more comprehensive reporting frameworks that move beyond traditional governance and safety considerations to encompass the broader social, ethical, and environmental impacts of AI. The analysis is directly related to the evolving landscape of the EU AI Act and aims to provide proactive insights into potential serious incidents. It suggests that a balanced approach to reporting is crucial, incorporating diverse perspectives and addressing risks across multiple dimensions. The document highlights the importance of continuous monitoring and adaptation of reporting mechanisms to ensure a holistic understanding of AI risks and their potential consequences. The data presented underscores the need for clearer definitions and stronger reporting structures to effectively manage AI risks and safeguard public safety. The report’s findings are relevant to organizations and stakeholders involved in the development and deployment of AI systems, particularly those operating within the context of regulatory frameworks like the EU AI Act.
