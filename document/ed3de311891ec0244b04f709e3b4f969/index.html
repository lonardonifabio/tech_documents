The Levers of Political Persuasion with Conversational Artificial Intelligence

Authors: Kobi Hackenburg, Ben M. Tappin, Luke Hewitt, Ed Saunders, Sid Black, Hause Lin, Catherine Fist, Helen Margetts, David G. Rand, Christopher Summerfield

This research article, published in Science on December 4, 2025, investigates the mechanisms behind the persuasive capabilities of large language models (LLMs) in the context of political influence. The study addresses a critical gap in understanding how conversational AI can potentially manipulate public opinion on political issues. Despite considerable speculation, the core factors driving the persuasiveness of LLMs – specifically, computational power (model scale), specialized post-training methods designed for persuasion, personalization tailored to individual users, and the incorporation of instructed rhetorical strategies – have remained largely unexplored. The research team systematically investigated these ‘levers’ through three large-scale experiments involving 76,977 UK participants. During these experiments, nineteen different LLMs were deployed to engage in conversations and attempt to persuade participants on a diverse range of 707 political issues. Crucially, the researchers varied the key factors mentioned above – model scale, post-training techniques, personalization, and rhetorical strategies – to assess their individual and combined impact on persuasiveness. The findings from these experiments provide valuable insights into the complex interplay of factors that determine the effectiveness of conversational AI in influencing political beliefs and opinions. The study highlights the importance of considering not just the raw power of LLMs, but also the strategic design and implementation of these models when attempting to persuade individuals on sensitive political topics. The research contributes significantly to the ongoing discussion about the ethical implications of AI and its potential impact on democratic processes. The data collected from these experiments will be crucial for future research aimed at developing safeguards and guidelines for the responsible development and deployment of conversational AI systems, particularly in areas where persuasion is involved.
