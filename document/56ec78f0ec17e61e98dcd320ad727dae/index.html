Deep Dive on Data and Information for Critical Infrastructure Management and Maintenance

Authors: Lloyd’s Register Foundation, The Alan Turing Institute, University of Strathclyde

This report, commissioned by Lloyd’s Register Foundation in partnership with The Alan Turing Institute and University of Strathclyde, focuses on leveraging data and information to enhance critical infrastructure management and maintenance. It addresses the significant challenge of building new infrastructure to meet future demands while simultaneously adapting to climate change and other planetary crises. The United Nations Environment Programme estimates that 75% of the infrastructure expected to exist by 2050 is yet to be constructed, highlighting the urgency of proactive management. The document emphasizes the increasing complexity and interconnectedness of modern infrastructure due to advanced digital technologies, necessitating robust maintenance strategies to protect personnel and supply chains. Data-driven approaches are presented as the key to preventing infrastructure failures. The report provides a framework for decision-makers in the infrastructure sector, outlining the importance of collecting the appropriate data, utilizing it for evidence-based decision-making, and applying data-centric engineering principles. Specifically, it targets the United Kingdom, reflecting Lloyd’s Register Foundation’s mission to ‘engineer a safer world.’ The core argument is that by strategically utilizing data, the resilience and reliability of critical infrastructure can be significantly improved, ensuring continued functionality and safety in the face of evolving challenges. The report’s collaborative origin – involving leading research institutions – underscores the importance of a multidisciplinary approach to addressing this complex issue. The document implicitly acknowledges the need for proactive investment and innovative solutions to manage and maintain the infrastructure required to support a growing population and mitigate the impacts of climate change.
