Runtime Delegation and Agentic AI Security

Authors: Unknown

This document outlines a framework for enhancing the security of agentic AI systems, specifically addressing the expanding adversarial surface introduced by runtime delegation. The core of the proposed approach centers around four key principles designed to ensure robust and adaptable security within a multi-agent environment. Firstly, **Observability** mandates the capture of all agent activity – including delegations, tool invocations, memory changes, and coordination events – in structured, queryable telemetry. This enables real-time oversight and facilitates post-event analysis, crucial for detecting and responding to potential threats. Secondly, **Bounded Autonomy** enforces strict operational boundaries for agents, limiting their actions to defined scopes based on role, context, and delegated authority. Crucially, these boundaries can be dynamically adjusted or revoked, preventing uncontrolled behavior and mitigating risks associated with unchecked delegation. Thirdly, **Ephemeral Identity** utilizes short-lived credentials and privileges tied to specific runtime contexts, automatically expiring and revoking upon expiry. This drastically reduces persistence and limits the potential for lateral movement in case of compromise. Finally, **Preserve Coordination Trust** focuses on securing inter-agent communication and shared memory through authentication, signing, and resistance to manipulation, disruption, or replay. Continuous monitoring of coordination pathways for behavioral drift and trust degradation further strengthens the system's resilience. The document frames agents as 'digital employees,' highlighting the relevance of these security practices to traditional operational security. The framework's design is applicable across various agentic AI deployments, emphasizing a proactive approach to security that adapts to the dynamic nature of these systems. The document implicitly suggests a need for continuous monitoring and adaptation to maintain security posture, given the inherent volatility of runtime delegation and the potential for evolving threats. The principles presented are designed to create a layered defense, combining technical controls with operational practices to safeguard agentic AI systems.
