Finland Country Profile: Preparing for the EU AI Act

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Turo Manninen, Minna Saonegin, Andrey Prozorov, Paavo Ritala

This document presents a country profile focusing on Finland's preparations for the EU Artificial Intelligence Act, released in January 2026. It details Finland's advancements in key areas crucial for responsible AI deployment, including data governance, high-performance computing, and adoption across sectors like healthcare, mobility, and the bioeconomy. The profile specifically highlights progress in establishing supervisory capabilities, addressing the unique challenges faced by small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), and developing robust conformity pathways. A central theme is the need for transparency and accountability within AI systems. The document identifies several key challenges that remain, such as expanding supervisory capacity to effectively oversee AI deployments, clarifying specific regulatory requirements for SMEs to encourage wider adoption, and strengthening existing conformity pathways to ensure consistent compliance. Furthermore, it emphasizes the importance of establishing shared standards to promote interoperability and reduce fragmentation within the Finnish AI landscape. The profile advocates for strategic investments in skills development, enhanced coordination among stakeholders, and the creation of shared standards to foster a more anticipatory, trustworthy, and resilient governance framework. The document also touches upon the broader implications of AI's increasing prevalence, raising the question of how AI-based solutions are already being utilized in various Finnish organizations, including in areas like recruitment, competence assessment, and employee experience. Ultimately, the document aims to illustrate Finland's proactive approach to navigating the complexities of the EU AI Act and leveraging AI's potential for societal benefit while mitigating potential risks.
