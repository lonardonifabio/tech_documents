AI Model Evaluations and Reporting

This document explores the evolving landscape of AI model evaluations and the associated reporting practices surrounding them. The research investigates a substantial dataset of 186 AI models, complemented by post-release reporting data sourced from 183 sources including websites and GitHub repositories. A key focus is on comparing first-party and third-party reporting, disaggregating results based on critical factors such as social impact categories, model openness (open vs. closed), sector of origin (academia, government, industry, non-profit), geographic region, and publication/release date. The analysis highlights the increasing importance of AI evaluations throughout the model lifecycle, influencing development, deployment, policy decisions, and practitioner choices. The document emphasizes that evaluation, defined as systematic assessment of model behavior in context, and benchmarks – standardized datasets, tasks, and metrics – are crucial for comparability. However, it critically points out a significant gap in evaluation rigor, noting that current practices often lack scientific methodology, transparency, and reproducibility. This deficiency results in insufficient information for further use and disparate methods across leaderboards. The research draws upon existing literature (Hardy et al., 2025; Biderman et al., 2024; Reuel-Lamparth et al., 2024; Summerfield et al., 2025; Chouldechova et al., 2024; Delobelle et al., 2024) to underscore the need for improved evaluation standards. Furthermore, the document defines and utilizes the term "foundation model," referring to pre-trained generative AI models (language-only or multimodal) lacking a specific application context. The research implicitly advocates for a more standardized and scientifically rigorous approach to AI model evaluation to ensure reliable comparisons and informed decision-making within the rapidly evolving field of artificial intelligence. The study's findings contribute to a growing body of work addressing the challenges of assessing and understanding the performance and impact of increasingly complex AI models.
