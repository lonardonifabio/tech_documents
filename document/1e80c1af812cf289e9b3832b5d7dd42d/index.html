Cyber AI Profile: Cybersecurity Framework Profile for Artificial Intelligence

Authors: Katerina Megas, Barbara Cuthill, Marissa Dotter, Michael Garris, Ishika Khemani, Bronwyn Patrick, Noah Schiro, Julie Nethery Snyder, Mohammad Zarei

This document, NIST Internal Report NIST IR 8596 iprd, presents an Initial Preliminary Draft of the ‘Cyber AI Profile,’ a Cybersecurity Framework Profile specifically tailored for Artificial Intelligence (AI). Developed by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), in collaboration with the Applied Cybersecurity Division of the Information Technology Laboratory, the National Cybersecurity Center of Excellence at MITRE, and the U.S. Department of Commerce, this report outlines a framework for understanding and addressing the unique cybersecurity risks and considerations associated with AI systems. The document details a preliminary approach to integrating AI into existing cybersecurity frameworks, focusing on areas such as data governance, model explainability, bias detection, and adversarial robustness. It’s intended as a starting point for further development and refinement of the Cyber AI Profile. The initial draft includes a community profile and specifies certain equipment, instruments, software, or materials used in the experimental procedure to adequately define the research. The report highlights the importance of a proactive and adaptive approach to cybersecurity in the age of increasingly sophisticated AI technologies. The document’s availability free of charge via DOI 10.6028/NIST.IR.8596.iprd underscores NIST’s commitment to open knowledge sharing and collaboration within the cybersecurity community. The draft was finalized in December 2025, with key contributors including Howard Lutnick (Secretary of Commerce), Craig Burkhardt (NIST Director), and Mohammad Zarei (formerly a contributor). The document’s purpose is to provide a foundational profile for cybersecurity considerations related to AI, acknowledging the evolving landscape and the need for ongoing research and development in this critical area. It serves as a crucial resource for organizations and researchers seeking to implement robust cybersecurity measures for AI systems.
