Czechia's AI Governance and Legal Framework

Authors: Unknown

This document outlines the legal and governance framework for Artificial Intelligence in Czechia, primarily relying on the EU AI Act's definition as its core reference. Czechia's approach is built around mitigating potential risks associated with AI, such as discrimination, privacy violations, and labor market disruptions, as detailed in the National Artificial Intelligence Strategy (NAIS) 2030. The NAIS, coordinated by the Ministry of Industry and Trade, emphasizes responsible AI development, aligning with international standards, and fostering inclusive economic growth. Several existing Czech laws play a crucial role in regulating AI's use and deployment, including the Civil Code, the Data Processing Act (aligned with GDPR), the Consumer Protection Act, the Copyright Act (specifically concerning text and data mining), the Anti-discrimination Act, the Competition Protection Act, the Cybersecurity Act, and national security regulations. These laws provide a foundational layer of oversight concerning data governance, consumer rights, and operational safety. The document highlights that Czechia’s AI governance is structured and innovation-driven, reflecting a commitment to international alignment. Furthermore, it’s explicitly linked to the EU’s Coordinated Plan for AI and incorporates broader national strategies like ‘Digital Czech Republic,’ indicating a strategic and coordinated approach to AI development and implementation. The document underscores the importance of stakeholder engagement, involving academia, industry, and civil society in the development and execution of the NAIS. The overall strategy aims to balance innovation with ethical considerations and legal compliance, ensuring a robust and adaptable framework for AI within the Czech Republic.
