What 30 0,000 Voices Tell Us About the Next Era of Global Change

Authors: Oliver Wyman Forum

This document excerpt outlines the findings and evolution of the Oliver Wyman Forum’s Global Voices survey, launched in 2020 to understand societal shifts following the initial impact of the global pandemic. The survey, which has collected data from nearly 300,000 individuals across 20 nations, aims to provide insights for business and government leaders navigating a period of unprecedented global change. The initial assumption was that the pandemic would create a ‘cohort memory,’ influencing long-term attitudes and beliefs, and this proved accurate. The survey reveals a significant shift in individual priorities, with people increasingly focused on personal fulfillment, trusting smaller communities over large institutions, and exhibiting a greater skepticism towards macro-level issues. There’s a noticeable trend of individuals feeling more empowered and thriving, although this doesn't always translate to a feeling of overall well-being. The data highlights concerns regarding disinformation, workforce trends, and geopolitical conflict, reflecting the multifaceted challenges of the 2020s. The ongoing nature of the survey, marked by its five-year milestone and continued data collection, underscores the dynamic and evolving nature of the global landscape and the importance of understanding public sentiment to inform strategic decision-making. The document emphasizes the value of gathering diverse perspectives – nearly 300,000 voices – to accurately assess the changes shaping the world and anticipate future trends. The survey’s methodology and its focus on capturing a broad range of opinions and experiences make it a valuable resource for those seeking to understand the complexities of the current era of global transformation. The core of the document is a reflection on the success of the Global Voices survey and its continued relevance in a world grappling with ongoing disruption and uncertainty.
