EU AI Act Switzerland Country Profile February 2026

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Dr. Martha Boeckenfeld, Yonah Welker, Robert Lienhard, German Ramirez

This document presents a country profile of Switzerland's preparations for the EU Artificial Intelligence Act, dated February 2026. It highlights Switzerland's advancements in key areas crucial for aligning with the upcoming regulations, specifically focusing on data governance, high-performance computing, and the responsible adoption of AI across vital sectors including healthcare, mobility, and the bioeconomy. The profile underscores Switzerland's proactive approach, recognizing the transformative impact of AI on regulation, innovation, and public sector capabilities. A central theme is the ongoing need to address several key challenges, notably expanding supervisory capacity to effectively oversee AI development and deployment, clarifying specific regulatory requirements for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) to foster inclusivity and understanding, and strengthening conformity pathways to ensure adherence to standards. Furthermore, the document emphasizes the importance of bolstering transparency within AI systems to build public trust and confidence. Switzerland's strategy involves strategic investments in developing a skilled workforce, fostering effective coordination among stakeholders, and establishing shared standards to guide AI development. The profile specifically notes Switzerland's emergence as a burgeoning ‘Silicon Valley of robotics,’ fueled by a dynamic startup ecosystem (over 30 companies), significant research contributions from institutions like EPFL, and the presence of established companies such as ANYbotics. Crucially, Switzerland is actively cultivating sovereign AI capabilities, leveraging cutting-edge infrastructure, most notably ETH Zurich’s Apertus open platform, which supports this endeavor. The document paints a picture of a nation strategically positioning itself at the forefront of AI innovation and responsible governance, anticipating the requirements of the EU AI Act and aiming to harness AI's potential for resilient and trustworthy governance. The focus on robotics alongside AI further solidifies Switzerland's position as a technologically advanced nation.
