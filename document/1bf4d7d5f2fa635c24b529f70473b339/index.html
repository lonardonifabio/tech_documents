Tech-tonic shifts: How AI could change industry risk landscapes

Authors: Swiss Re Institute

This report, published by the Swiss Re Institute in May 2024, investigates the evolving risk landscape associated with the increasing adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) across various industries. The study employs a novel approach, combining text analysis of recent AI incidents, historical loss data, and forward-looking patent applications to generate risk ratings for both short- and medium-term durations. The core methodology focuses on establishing frequency and severity weightings for different sectors, providing a nuanced understanding of potential AI-related risks. The analysis reveals that the technology sector currently presents the highest risk profile, understandably due to its central role in AI development. However, the report anticipates a comparatively lower risk probability in the energy and utilities sectors, although incidents within these sectors are projected to carry significant severity, particularly as AI is integrated into smart grids, increasing the frequency of potential events. A key area of immediate concern identified is the media and communications sector, which faces short-term exposure to intellectual property risks stemming from the emergence of generative AI and the ongoing legal debates surrounding the use of copyrighted materials in training large language models (LLMs). The study’s findings suggest a shift in risk distribution as AI becomes more prevalent, with healthcare and pharmaceuticals emerging as prominent risk areas due to the sector's reliance on data and the potential for AI-driven innovations. The research utilizes a comprehensive data set, including incident analysis and patent data, to provide a robust and forward-looking assessment of AI risk. The report concludes with implications for insurers, highlighting the need for proactive risk management strategies in the face of this rapidly changing technological landscape. The appendix details the data sources and methodology employed, further strengthening the credibility of the Institute’s findings. Ultimately, this document serves as a critical analysis for understanding and mitigating the emerging risks posed by AI across a range of industries, particularly for those involved in insurance and risk assessment.
