Artificial Intelligence and Civil Liability: A European Perspective

Authors: Andrea BERTOLINI

This study, commissioned by the European Parliament's Committee on Legal Affairs, critically examines the complex issue of civil liability concerning artificial intelligence systems within the European Union. The core objective is to prevent regulatory fragmentation across Member States, a significant challenge in the rapidly evolving field of AI. The document strongly advocates for a strict liability regime specifically targeted at high-risk AI systems. This regime would be centrally managed by a single, designated responsible operator, ensuring clarity and accountability. A key element of the proposed approach is the establishment of legal certainty, aiming to streamline processes and reduce ambiguity. Furthermore, the study emphasizes the importance of efficiency and harmonization, recognizing that a consistent legal framework is crucial for fostering innovation and responsible AI development. The research underscores the need for a robust legal foundation to govern AI systems, mitigating potential risks and promoting public trust. The document’s findings are intended to inform policy discussions and contribute to the development of effective regulatory strategies within the European Union. The study’s recommendations are designed to balance the benefits of AI with the need for appropriate safeguards, ultimately shaping a future where AI systems operate within a clear and predictable legal environment. The research highlights the importance of proactive measures to address liability concerns, ensuring that AI systems are developed and deployed responsibly.
