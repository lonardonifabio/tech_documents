Artificial Intelligence, Human Rights, Democracy, and the Rule of Law

Authors: David Leslie, Christopher Burr, Mhairi Aitken, Josh Cowls, Mike Ketell, Morgan Briggs, Lord Tim Clement-Jones

This document, a primer produced by The Alan Turing Institute's Public Policy Programme, explores the intersection of artificial intelligence with fundamental concepts like human rights, democracy, and the rule of law. Established in May 2018, the program’s core mission is to assist governments in leveraging data-intensive technologies while prioritizing ethical considerations and safety. The primer serves as a living document, intended to evolve through user feedback and stakeholder input. It emphasizes the importance of governments proactively addressing the ethical implications of AI to ensure responsible innovation and improved public services. The research is supported by grants from the ESRC (ES/T007354/1) and public funds, highlighting a commitment to collaborative research and development. Importantly, the opinions expressed are those of the authors and do not represent the official policy of the Council of Europe. The document’s creation reflects a broader effort to guide policy makers in navigating the complex challenges and opportunities presented by artificial intelligence, advocating for a human-centered approach to technological advancement. The ongoing development of this primer underscores the dynamic nature of this field and the need for continuous dialogue and adaptation. The document’s purpose is to stimulate discussion and contribute to a framework for responsible AI governance.
