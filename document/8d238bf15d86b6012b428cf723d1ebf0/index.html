CS229 Machine Learning Handout #1: Course Information

Authors: Andrew Ng, Paul Baumstarck, Catie Chang, Chuong (Tom) Do, Zico Kolter, Daniel Ramage

This document provides course information for CS229 Machine Learning, taught by Professor Andrew Ng and a team of Teaching Assistants (TAs) including Paul Baumstarck, Catie Chang, Chuong (Tom) Do, Zico Kolter, and Daniel Ramage. The course offers a broad introduction to machine learning and statistical pattern recognition, covering a wide range of topics within the field. Key areas of study include supervised learning techniques such as generative and discriminative learning, parametric and non-parametric approaches, neural networks, and support vector machines. Unsupervised learning methods like clustering, dimensionality reduction, and kernel methods are also explored. The course delves into learning theory, specifically addressing bias/variance tradeoffs, VC theory, and large margins. Furthermore, it examines reinforcement learning and adaptive control. Recent applications of machine learning are discussed, spanning diverse fields such as robotic control, data mining, autonomous navigation, bioinformatics, speech recognition, and processing of text and web data. Prerequisites for the course include a foundational understanding of computer science principles and programming skills, along with familiarity with basic probability theory and linear algebra. While no required textbook is specified, the course materials consist of handouts and lectures. The course aims to provide students with a comprehensive understanding of the core concepts and techniques in machine learning, preparing them for further study or application in various domains. The course structure is designed to build a strong theoretical base alongside practical applications, fostering a deep understanding of the underlying principles.
