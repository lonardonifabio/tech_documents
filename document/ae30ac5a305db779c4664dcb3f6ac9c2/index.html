Guideline on the Use of AI in Arbitration (2025)

Authors: Cristen Bauer, Claire Morel de Westgaver, Amy Endicott, Annabelle Onyefulu, Carlos Carvalho, Fabio Solimene, Harry Borovick, Karolina Jackowicz, Kateryna Honcharenko, Matthew Lavy KC, Maud Piers Meriam Al Rashid, Peter Neumann FCIArb

This document presents a guideline outlining the appropriate use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) within the context of arbitration proceedings. Published in 2025, the guideline is a collaborative effort developed by The Chartered Institute of Arbitrators (CIArb) through its Policy and Professional Practice Team and the Technology Thought Leadership Group’s AI Guideline Drafting Committee. The primary objective is to provide a framework for arbitrators and parties to navigate the emerging challenges and opportunities presented by AI technologies in dispute resolution. It addresses key considerations such as transparency, bias mitigation, data security, and the potential impact of AI on the fairness and efficiency of arbitration processes. The guideline emphasizes the importance of human oversight and judgment, recognizing that AI should be used as a tool to augment, rather than replace, the core principles of arbitration. It likely delves into specific applications of AI, including predictive analytics, document review, and evidence analysis, while also acknowledging the need for robust safeguards to ensure impartiality and accountability. The document’s creation reflects a proactive approach by CIArb to address the rapidly evolving landscape of AI and its implications for the legal profession. The inclusion of multiple authors from diverse backgrounds – including CIArb members with varying qualifications (MCIArb, FCIArb, KC) – suggests a comprehensive and well-considered approach. Furthermore, the copyright notice and disclaimer highlight the authors' rights and CIArb's position as the source of the guideline, emphasizing that views expressed are those of the authors unless specifically endorsed by CIArb. The document's focus on ethical considerations and practical guidance makes it a valuable resource for anyone involved in arbitration seeking to understand and utilize AI responsibly.
