Cyber Resilience Act versus EU AI Act A Mapping Exercise

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Dr. Amritha Subhayan Krishnan, Arnoud Engelfriet, Indra Joshi, Maryam Ghadrdan (PhD), Debbie Reyno

This report, published by AI & Partners, provides a mapping exercise comparing the Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) and the EU AI Act. AI & Partners, a firm dedicated to defending digital rights and promoting responsible AI development, produced this document. The organization’s core mission is to ensure AI systems are built and utilized in a way that fosters trust and innovation, mitigating the risks associated with uncontrolled AI deployments. The report likely delves into the key differences and potential overlaps between these two regulatory frameworks, focusing on how the CRA’s focus on cybersecurity and risk management interacts with the EU AI Act’s broader approach to governing AI systems. AI & Partners advocates for a proactive stance, emphasizing the importance of responsible AI development to avoid negative consequences like confusion and loss of control. The firm’s approach combines technical support, policy engagement, advocacy, and regulatory interventions. The document highlights the organization's belief that AI should unlock potential rather than cause harm, and they aim to assist organizations in building AI systems they can trust and stand behind. The report’s purpose is to provide a framework for understanding and navigating these evolving regulatory landscapes, ultimately contributing to the safe and ethical development and deployment of AI technologies.
