Why GenAI is More Likely to Support Workers Than Replace Them

Authors: Annina Hering, Ph.D., Arcenis Rojas

This report, titled "Why GenAI is More Likely to Support Workers Than Replace Them," is based on an analysis of over 2,800 unique work skills conducted by Indeed. The key finding is that none of these skills are ‘very likely’ to be replaced by generative artificial intelligence. The research was conducted by Annina Hering, a senior economist at Indeed Hiring Lab and principal researcher for the report, and Arcenis Rojas, a data scientist at Hiring Lab. Hering’s background includes a Ph.D. in social science from the University of Cologne, postdoctoral research at the Max Planck Institute for the Study of Societies (IMPRS-SPCE), and degrees in Sociology and Empirical Research. Rojas holds a Bachelor’s in Applied Economics from Ithaca College and a Master’s in Economics from the City University of New York – the City College. The report highlights a shift in the perception of AI, suggesting it will primarily augment human capabilities rather than automate jobs entirely. The research methodology involved a comprehensive assessment of work skills, indicating a cautious approach to the impact of GenAI on the workforce. The authors’ diverse backgrounds – encompassing economics, data science, and social science – contribute to a well-rounded perspective on the evolving relationship between AI and employment.
