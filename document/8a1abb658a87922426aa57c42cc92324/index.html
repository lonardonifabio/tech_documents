Making Europe an AI Continent

Authors: Maria Niestadt, Members' Research Service

This briefing paper, produced by the European Parliamentary Research Service, analyzes the European Union's strategy to establish itself as a leading ‘AI continent.’ Released in September 2025 (PE 775.923), the document highlights the significance of the 2024 Artificial Intelligence Act as a foundational step, but emphasizes that regulation alone is insufficient. The core of the paper focuses on the European Commission’s ‘AI continent action plan,’ published in April 2025, which moves beyond solely regulatory oversight. This plan incorporates investment, infrastructure development, and skills training initiatives to drive AI adoption across both the private and public sectors. The briefing underscores the EU's strategic shift towards prioritizing competitiveness, departing from a previous emphasis on prescriptive usage rules. However, the document acknowledges significant challenges, including the EU’s relative lag in AI scale, investment levels, and the adoption of AI technologies compared to global leaders. Key structural obstacles identified include a fragmented single market, constrained private investment, and a dependence on foreign cloud computing and semiconductor technologies. The document also notes the divergent viewpoints among stakeholders. Industry advocates champion simplified regulations to stimulate innovation, while civil society expresses concerns about potential compromises to democratic values and safeguards. The briefing ultimately paints a picture of a complex and ongoing effort, with the EU striving to overcome substantial hurdles to achieve its ambitious goal of becoming a dominant force in the global AI landscape. The document serves as a strategic overview of the EU's approach, outlining the key components and acknowledging the inherent difficulties.
