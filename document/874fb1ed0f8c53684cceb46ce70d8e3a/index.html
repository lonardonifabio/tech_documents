Discussion Paper on Draft Recommendation on AI Literacy

Authors: Wayne Holmes

This document presents a discussion paper outlining a draft recommendation on AI literacy, prepared by a Council of Europe consultant, Wayne Holmes. The core argument centers around the increasing need to address AI literacy, building upon the Council of Europe’s Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights (2024), which emphasizes digital literacy and skills related to AI systems. Specifically, Article 20 of the Convention highlights the responsibility of member states to foster digital literacy and skills, particularly concerning the identification, assessment, prevention, and mitigation of risks associated with AI. The paper acknowledges the recognition of ‘AI literacy’ by international bodies such as the European Union, UNESCO, and the OECD, exemplified by the EU AI Act (2024) which mandates that providers and deployers of AI systems ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy among their staff and those involved in operating these systems. The document recognizes that while AI literacy can be considered a subset of digital literacy, it deserves distinct attention due to the human-like nature of AI systems. The discussion paper likely delves into the specific components of AI literacy, including understanding the design, development, use, and decommissioning of AI systems, as well as the potential risks and ethical considerations surrounding their deployment. It’s a foundational document exploring the critical need for widespread AI literacy to ensure responsible and beneficial use of AI technologies within the context of human rights, democracy, and the rule of law. The paper’s purpose is to inform the development of a formal recommendation, suggesting a proactive approach to equipping individuals with the knowledge and skills necessary to navigate the evolving landscape of artificial intelligence.
