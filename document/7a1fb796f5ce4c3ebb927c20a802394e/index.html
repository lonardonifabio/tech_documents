Persuading Voters Using Human–Artificial Intelligence Dialogues

Authors: Hause Lin, Gabriela Czarnek, Benjamin Lewis, Joshua P. White, Adam J. Berinsky, Thomas Costello, Gordon Pennycook, David G. Rand

This research investigates the potential of large language models to influence voter attitudes and opinions, addressing growing public concerns regarding the use of generative AI in political persuasion. The study, pre-registered, utilizes experiments conducted in the context of simulated elections – specifically the 2024 US presidential election, the 2025 Canadian federal election, and the 2025 Polish presidential election – to assess the impact of conversations with AI models advocating for leading candidates. Participants were randomly assigned to engage in dialogues with these AI models, and the results revealed significant treatment effects on candidate preference, exceeding those typically observed with traditional video advertisements. Notably, the AI models primarily employed relevant facts and evidence in their persuasive arguments, rather than relying on complex psychological manipulation techniques. However, a key finding highlighted a concerning trend: AI models advocating for candidates on the political right exhibited a higher frequency of inaccurate claims across all three countries. This suggests a potential vulnerability in AI-driven persuasion campaigns and raises important questions about the accuracy and reliability of information presented by these systems. The research contributes to a broader understanding of the challenges posed by AI in the political sphere, particularly concerning the potential for manipulation and the need for safeguards to ensure informed decision-making. The study's findings underscore the importance of critical evaluation of information sources, especially in an era where AI-generated content is increasingly prevalent. The research also touches upon the broader implications for democratic processes and the need for ongoing scrutiny of the use of AI in political campaigns.
