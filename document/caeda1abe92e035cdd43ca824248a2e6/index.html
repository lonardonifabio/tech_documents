This research paper, "The AI Model Risk Catalog," investigates the reporting of risks associated with AI models, specifically focusing on the discrepancies between how developers and researchers approach identifying potential harms. The authors analyzed a substantial dataset of approximately 460,000 AI model cards sourced from Hugging Face, extracting around 3,000 unique risk mentions to construct the AI Model Risk Catalog. They compared these identified risks with those documented in the MIT Risk Repository and real-world AI incident data. The analysis revealed a significant gap in attention, with developers primarily concentrating on technical aspects of AI model risks such as bias and safety, while researchers tended to emphasize broader social impacts. A key finding was the consistent underreporting of fraud and manipulation risks – harms frequently arising from human interaction with AI systems. The study highlights a critical need for improved, more structured risk reporting frameworks that encourage developers to proactively consider human-interaction and systemic risks during the initial design stages of AI models. The research underscores the importance of a holistic approach to AI safety and ethics, moving beyond purely technical considerations to encompass the wider societal implications. The authors advocate for a shift in focus, urging developers to incorporate human-centered design principles and a deeper understanding of potential misuse scenarios. The catalog and accompanying paper provide a valuable resource for researchers, developers, and policymakers seeking to address the complex risks posed by increasingly prevalent AI systems. The availability of the catalog and supporting materials at the provided URL facilitates further exploration and collaboration within the field. This work contributes to a growing body of literature addressing the ethical and practical challenges of deploying AI responsibly, emphasizing the need for a more comprehensive and nuanced understanding of potential harms.
