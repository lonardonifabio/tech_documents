Preparing for AI Agent Governance

Authors: Jacob Pratt, Thalia Khan

This research document, "Preparing for AI Agent Governance," outlines a research agenda specifically targeted towards policymakers and researchers. It addresses the critical need for proactive governance frameworks surrounding the emerging technology of AI agents. The core focus is on understanding the technological nuances of AI agents, differentiating them from existing AI systems, and mapping relevant legal and policy landscapes across various jurisdictions. The document identifies key policy questions that require further investigation, including how AI agents are likely to be utilized, the potential benefits and risks associated with their adoption, and the mechanisms for market correction. It delves into potential policy interventions, such as regulatory sandboxes, visibility and monitoring initiatives, and incentive structures designed to foster beneficial AI adoption. A significant portion of the research centers on the implementation of these interventions, exploring approaches like licensing, audit, and assurance-related measures. The document emphasizes the importance of monitoring, sandboxes, and testbeds as priorities for policymakers. Furthermore, it highlights PAI’s specific focus on AI agents, suggesting a strategic direction for research and policy development in this rapidly evolving field. The document’s overall aim is to provide a roadmap for policymakers and researchers to navigate the complexities of AI agent governance, ensuring responsible innovation and mitigating potential risks. It’s a forward-looking analysis, recognizing the need for adaptable and proactive strategies to manage the societal impact of AI agents.
