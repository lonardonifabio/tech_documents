The State of State AI: Legislative Approaches to AI in 2025

Authors: Justine Gluck, Beth Do, Tatiana Rice, Bridget Egan

This report, published by the Future of Privacy Forum (FPF) in October 2025, analyzes the evolving landscape of state-level AI legislation. It focuses on the trends observed in bills related to artificial intelligence that were either enrolled, enacted, or advanced during the year 2025. The core finding is a shift away from broad, sweeping AI regulations towards more targeted and transparency-focused approaches. Specifically, the report identifies three dominant regulatory strategies: 1) Use and context-specific regulations, concentrating on sensitive applications of AI; 2) Technology-specific regulations, designed to address particular aspects of AI technology; and 3) A liability and accountability approach, which involves adapting existing legal frameworks to cover AI-related risks and responsibilities. The report highlights the increasing prevalence of these strategies among state lawmakers. The Future of Privacy Forum (FPF), a non-profit organization dedicated to advancing principled data practices, produced this analysis as part of its ongoing efforts to shape AI policy. The document serves as a valuable resource for policymakers, legal professionals, and anyone interested in understanding the current state of AI regulation at the state level. It underscores the importance of a nuanced and adaptable approach to AI governance, recognizing the diverse applications and potential risks associated with this rapidly evolving technology. The report’s findings are relevant to the broader discussion surrounding responsible AI development and deployment, emphasizing the need for clear guidelines and frameworks that promote innovation while safeguarding privacy and ethical considerations. Further research and analysis are needed to fully assess the long-term impact of these legislative trends.
