MDR versus EU AI Act: A Mapping Exercise

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Teddy Ramanakasina, Harvey Castro, Joris Krijger, Donald John Musch, Michael Charles Borrelli

This report, published by AI & Partners, provides a comparative analysis between the existing MDR (General Data Protection Regulation) and the proposed EU AI Act, slated for implementation in August 2025. The document highlights AI & Partners' mission to defend digital rights for users globally, particularly those at risk, through a multi-faceted approach encompassing technical support, policy engagement, advocacy, and regulatory interventions. The core argument centers around the need for responsible AI development and deployment, recognizing the potential for harm when AI systems are not properly controlled. AI & Partners emphasizes the importance of trust and innovation in the AI age, advocating for tools and strategies to identify, monitor, and govern AI systems to mitigate confusion, risk, and reputational damage associated with unknown AI adoption. The report positions AI & Partners as a key player in navigating the evolving regulatory landscape surrounding AI, offering services to help organizations and their clients build and manage AI systems with a focus on long-term sustainability and ethical considerations. It underscores the critical need for proactive governance to ensure AI benefits society rather than causing unintended consequences. The document’s purpose is to map the differences between the MDR and the EU AI Act, suggesting that the MDR may not adequately address the specific challenges posed by advanced AI systems, necessitating a more robust regulatory framework like the EU AI Act. The report’s authors, AI & Partners, aim to guide organizations through this transition, offering expertise and support to ensure compliance and responsible AI practices. The document’s focus on user rights and responsible AI aligns with the broader movement towards ethical AI development and deployment, reflecting a growing concern about the potential societal impacts of this rapidly evolving technology. Ultimately, the report serves as a call to action for organizations to prioritize transparency, accountability, and ethical considerations in their AI initiatives.
