AI Incident Response: Adapting Proven Complex Systems Engineering Practices for AI-Enabled Systems

Authors: Heather Frase

This document, authored by Heather Frase of Veraitec, explores the critical need for a new approach to incident response specifically tailored for AI-enabled systems. Published in November 2025, it argues that current incident response methodologies are insufficient for addressing the unique complexities and risks associated with AI. The core of the document centers around a seven-step process grounded in proven complex systems engineering practices, designed to provide a structured framework for managing and mitigating AI incidents. It identifies significant gaps in existing capabilities, stemming from the inherent complexity of AI-enabled systems and a lack of foundational structures. The document advocates for an ‘ecosystem approach,’ emphasizing the importance of coordinated collaboration between various stakeholders – including AI deployers, developers, assurance organizations, and standards bodies. Specifically, it outlines a detailed seven-step incident response loop, focusing on proactive monitoring, rapid diagnosis, containment, and recovery. Furthermore, it delves into the distinct characteristics of AI incidents, differentiating them from traditional system failures. The document proposes actionable recommendations for each key stakeholder group, aiming to foster a more robust and effective AI incident response strategy. It highlights the multiplicative benefits of ecosystem coordination and stresses the need for integrating this framework with existing assurance and audit processes. The core argument is that by adapting established complex systems engineering principles, organizations can significantly improve their ability to handle and resolve incidents within AI-driven environments, ultimately enhancing system reliability and minimizing potential disruptions. The document’s publication date of November 2025 suggests a forward-looking perspective on the evolving challenges of AI systems.

Key Concepts:

  • The urgency of AI Incident Response due to the increasing complexity and potential impact of AI systems.
  • A framework for AI Incident Response grounded in proven complex systems engineering practices, utilizing a seven-step process.
  • The importance of ecosystem coordination involving key stakeholders (deployers, developers, assurance organizations, standards bodies) to maximize the benefits of AI Incident Response.
  • Development of technical frameworks for incident reporting.
  • Need for a mandatory reporting regime for AI incidents.
  • Importance of preparedness infrastructure and standardized reporting in responding to AI incidents.
  • Systematic incident response is needed to address both tangible and intangible harms.
  • Hazards are conditions that could lead to harm but haven't caused it, and organizations should proactively address them.
  • Vulnerabilities are weaknesses in AI systems that could be exploited, and a prompt injection vulnerability exists regardless of exploitation.
  • New challenges for incident response due to expanding AI capabilities.
