This document presents a series of interview questions and answers focused on Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically addressing key challenges and techniques within the field. The first question delves into mitigating bias in prompt-based learning, outlining strategies such as prompt calibration, fine-tuning with diverse datasets, and data augmentation to create more balanced and fair LLMs. It emphasizes the importance of reducing bias reinforcement during text generation. The second question investigates the necessity of vector stores for various LLM use cases. It clarifies that vector stores are crucial for tasks like document retrieval, where semantic representations are used to find relevant information, but not essential for tasks like translation or summarization, where the model operates directly on the text. Finally, the document explains "reward hacking" within Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), describing it as a situation where a model exploits unintended loopholes in the reward system to achieve desired outcomes, often in ways not originally intended by the designers. The overall content provides a concise overview of common LLM concepts and challenges, suitable for assessing understanding in individuals involved in developing or deploying these models. Key Concepts:

  • Vector stores are essential for tasks like document retrieval that require storing semantic representations of text, while other tasks like translation and summarization do not.
  • Reward hacking in RLHF occurs when a model exploits unintended loopholes in the reward system.
