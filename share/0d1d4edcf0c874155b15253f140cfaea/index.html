Draft Guidance Article 73 AI Act - Incident Reporting (High -Risk AI Systems)

Authors: European Commission

This document presents draft guidance related to the reporting of incidents involving high-risk AI systems, specifically in the context of the European Commission's proposed AI Act. The core objective is to establish a framework for systematically identifying, documenting, and reporting adverse events stemming from the deployment or operation of AI systems classified as high-risk. The guidance aims to ensure accountability and transparency within the AI ecosystem, mitigating potential harms and fostering responsible innovation. It begins with a background outlining the need for robust incident reporting, particularly given the potential impact of high-risk AI systems on society and individuals. A key section defines critical terms, including 'AI Act,' 'Incident,' and 'Malfunction,' establishing a common understanding for stakeholders. The document meticulously dissects the concept of 'directly or indirectly' caused incidents, clarifying the scope of responsibility. The guidance emphasizes the importance of thorough investigation and documentation of incidents, regardless of whether they are immediately apparent. It’s designed to support organizations in complying with the forthcoming AI Act, promoting proactive risk management and facilitating effective responses to unforeseen circumstances. The document serves as a foundational resource for understanding the reporting requirements associated with high-risk AI systems, contributing to a safer and more trustworthy AI landscape. The draft guidance is intended to be a living document, subject to further refinement and updates as the AI Act progresses through the legislative process. It’s crucial for organizations developing or deploying high-risk AI systems to familiarize themselves with these reporting procedures to ensure compliance and minimize potential liabilities. The document's ultimate goal is to establish a standardized approach to incident reporting, enabling effective monitoring, analysis, and ultimately, the prevention of future harm.

Key Concepts:

  • The document outlines draft guidance related to the AI Act's incident reporting requirements for high-risk AI systems.
  • The document defines key terms such as 'AI Act' and 'Incident and Malfunction'.
  • The guidance focuses on determining whether an incident was 'directly or indirectly' caused by an AI system.
  • Incident definition is unclear, leading to inconsistent reporting.
  • Causal relationship between incident and harm is crucial for reporting.
  • Direct and indirect causation are considered when assessing incidents.
  • The AI Act focuses on serious harms caused by high-risk AI systems.
