A Nationally Representative Survey of Public Attitudes to Artificial Intelligence in Britain

Authors: Ada Lovelace Institute, The Alan Turing Institute

This document presents the findings of a nationally representative survey conducted in Britain to assess public attitudes towards artificial intelligence (AI). The survey, undertaken in November 2022, involved over 4,000 adults and aimed to understand public awareness, experiences, and opinions regarding various AI applications. Researchers explored perceptions of AI's advantages and disadvantages, alongside desired regulatory frameworks. A key focus was on addressing the ambiguity surrounding the term ‘AI’ itself, recognizing its broad application across diverse technologies and contexts. The study highlights the significant national attention AI is receiving due to its rapid development and the resulting policy discussions. The research acknowledges the challenges in defining AI due to its multifaceted nature and the varying ways it's utilized. The survey data provides valuable insights into public sentiment, which is crucial for informing responsible AI development and governance. The report details the methodology used, key findings, and a conclusion summarizing the overall public perception of AI in Britain. The survey's findings are intended to contribute to a more nuanced understanding of public attitudes and to guide policy decisions related to AI’s deployment and oversight. The document includes an executive summary, a guide on how to read the report, an introduction, methodology, key findings, and an appendix.

Key Concepts:
