Bulgaria's Approach to AI: A Hybrid Framework

Authors: Unknown

This document analyzes Bulgaria's approach to Artificial Intelligence (AI), highlighting its unique position within the European Union's evolving regulatory landscape. Bulgaria currently lacks dedicated, standalone AI legislation, operating instead under a hybrid model that integrates the EU AI Act alongside sector-specific national laws concerning e-administration, data protection, and copyright. This approach reflects a commitment to aligning with broader European values and promoting ethical AI development and deployment across both public and private sectors. The document emphasizes the crucial role of national initiatives focused on ethical AI, incorporating oversight, transparency, and responsible implementation. Specifically, the National Authority for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (NAIH) is central to enforcing data protection regulations pertinent to AI applications, while other authorities – those managing copyright and e-administration – contribute to the overall governance of AI systems within their respective domains. The absence of specific AI legislation necessitates a reliance on existing legal frameworks, which are being adapted and interpreted to address the unique challenges posed by AI technologies. The document underscores the importance of ongoing guidance and evolving sectoral governance tools to ensure compliance with ethical and legal standards. Furthermore, the Bulgarian AI strategy is explicitly designed to align with EU values, establishing principles for AI development and deployment. The framework’s success hinges on the collaborative efforts of various regulatory bodies and a continuous process of adaptation to emerging AI technologies and best practices. The document’s core argument is that Bulgaria is proactively managing the risks and opportunities of AI through a layered approach, prioritizing ethical considerations and legal compliance within a dynamic regulatory environment. The integration of the EU AI Act, alongside existing national laws, represents a pragmatic strategy for navigating the complexities of AI governance, particularly in the absence of bespoke legislation.

Key Concepts:

  • Bulgaria’s AI research output exhibits a long-term upward trend with volatility.
  • Significant growth in AI publications occurred after 2012, peaking in 2019.
  • The trend is influenced by integration into European research networks, EU Framework Programmes, and national digital modernisation strategies.
  • Bulgaria lacks standalone AI legislation but follows a hybrid model combining the EU AI Act and sector -specific national laws.
  • National initiatives support ethical AI aligned wit h EU values, ensuring oversight, transparency, and responsible deployment across public and private sectors.
  • The National Authority for Data Protection and Freedom of Information (NAIH) plays a crucial role in enforcing data protection aspects relevant to AI.
  • The government's strategy focuses on utilizing AI within public services to enhance efficiency and tackle societal issues.
  • Bulgaria's investment in advanced computing infrastructure, including high-performance and distributed computing, is crucial for AI development.
  • Supporting innovation and technology transfer, particularly for SMEs and startups, is a key priority to facilitate AI implementation.
