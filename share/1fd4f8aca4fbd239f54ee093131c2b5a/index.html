Exploratory Data Analysis of Netflix Movies Dataset

Authors: Unknown

This document excerpt details an exploratory data analysis (EDA) project focused on a Netflix movie dataset sourced from Kaggle. The dataset comprises 16 years of records, offering granular information on movies including title, director, release year, genre, duration, country of production, and ratings. The primary objective of the analysis is to uncover key patterns and insights within the data, specifically examining the popularity, ratings, and financial success of movies across various categories. The analysis delves into genres, countries, languages, and directors to determine which categories drive audience engagement and box office performance. Furthermore, the project utilizes global metrics like ratings, popularity, budget, and revenue to identify overarching trends and correlations. The analysis leverages Python libraries such as NumPy, Pandas, Matplotlib, and Seaborn for data manipulation, visualization, and statistical exploration. The initial data structure is examined, showcasing columns like show_id, title, director, cast, country, date_added, and release_year. The project aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of Netflix's movie portfolio and the factors influencing its success, ultimately contributing to insights into audience preferences and market trends. The use of a Kaggle dataset suggests a common practice in data science education and research, providing a readily available and diverse dataset for analysis.

Key Concepts:

  • Identification of key patterns and insights from Netflix movie data.
  • Analysis of movie genres, countries, languages, and directors to determine popularity and success.
  • Examination of global metrics (ratings, popularity, budget, revenue) to understand overall movie performance.
  • Genre-wise analysis
  • Grouping by genres
  • Calculating metrics (number_of_movies, avg_rating, etc.)
  • Sorting by metrics
  • Top 10 genre ranking
  • Visualization of genre popularity using bar plots
