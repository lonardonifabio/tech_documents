Sustaining Slovenia’s AI Talent Momentum: Strategic Priorities to Convert Steady Gains into a Robust, Competitive, and Innovation - Driven Ecosystem by 2030

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Kristina Kočet Hudrap, Sara Kremsar, Milovan Tomašević, Aljaz (Al) Peklaj, DAVID HORVAT, Matija Jamnik, Klemen Selakovič, Sasa Sodja, Andrej P. Skraba

This document, titled "Sustaining Slovenia’s AI Talent Momentum," presents an AI Ecosystem Brief focusing on Slovenia’s progress in developing an AI-skilled workforce by 2030. The core analysis centers around a chart illustrating the concentration of LinkedIn members possessing AI engineering skills or holding AI-related occupations within Slovenia from 2016 to 2024. The data reveals a consistently upward trend, starting at 0.322 in 2016 and reaching 0.715 in 2024. Notably, Slovenia’s growth pattern contrasts with more volatile AI talent concentrations observed in other countries, indicating a stable and deliberate approach to capacity building. The document attributes this success to several key factors: robust educational pipelines, targeted national innovation initiatives, and active participation in broader European Union digital programs. A significant acceleration in talent concentration is observed post-2020, suggesting a positive impact of recent strategic interventions. The brief highlights the importance of maintaining this momentum to establish Slovenia as a competitive and innovative AI ecosystem. The analysis emphasizes the need for continued investment and strategic planning to sustain the upward trajectory of AI talent within the Slovenian economy. The document’s purpose is to provide strategic priorities for Slovenia to leverage its existing gains and solidify its position as a leader in AI development within the EU.

Key Concepts:

  • Slovenia’s AI talent concentration is steadily increasing from 2016 to 2024.
  • The growth in AI talent concentration is driven by stable educational pipelines, national innovation initiatives, and EU digital programs.
  • Slovenia’s AI ecosystem exhibits a consistent and gradual capacity building trend, contrasting with volatile patterns in other countries.
  • Slovenian transposition of the EU AI Act into domestic law.
  • Establishment of an expert council to advise on AI regulatory and policy issues.
  • Creation of a national AI regulatory sandbox for safe AI system testing and compliance.
  • Slovenia’s comprehensive approach to AI governance, deployment, and innovation is rooted in EU alignment, ethical safeguards, and institutional coordination.
  • The NpUI positions AI as a driver of social well-being, economic competitiveness, and public sector modernization, while centering trust, human-centricity, and international cooperation in its implementation.
  • The NpUI fosters a national AI ecosystem by linking universities, public research institutes, industrial R&D centers, and digital innovation hubs (DIHs).
