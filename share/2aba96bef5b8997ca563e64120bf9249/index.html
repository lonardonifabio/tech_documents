AI Governance: A Framework for Responsible and Compliant Artificial Intelligence

Authors: Anna Tujakowska, Sylwia Macura -Targosz, Agata Szeliga

This document, published in September 2025, presents a framework for responsible and compliant artificial intelligence governance. It addresses the increasing integration of AI into business processes and the associated risks, including legal, ethical, operational, and reputational concerns. The core of the framework emphasizes the need for cross-functional collaboration, involving legal and compliance teams to ensure regulatory alignment, data and IT personnel to manage AI infrastructure and model lifecycles, and business stakeholders. The document begins with an executive summary outlining the challenges and the importance of proactive governance. It then delves into the legal framework, specifically referencing the AI Act and personal data protection regulations, alongside other relevant regulations. A significant portion is dedicated to identifying and analyzing the risks associated with AI usage. These risks are categorized and discussed in detail. The framework proposes recommendations and best practices, categorized into transparency and engagement, AI literacy, security and robustness, and human oversight. It highlights the importance of building AI literacy within organizations and emphasizes the need for robust security measures and human involvement in AI systems. The document also acknowledges and addresses key challenges, such as organizational resistance, the balance between innovation and control, technical complexity, and the evolving and fragmented legal environment surrounding AI. A case study of Microsoft is presented as an example of good practices. Ultimately, the document aims to provide a practical guide for organizations seeking to harness the power of AI while mitigating its potential downsides, promoting responsible and compliant AI adoption. The framework underscores the need for a holistic approach to AI governance, recognizing that it's not solely the responsibility of one department but requires a coordinated effort across the entire organization.

Key Concepts:

  • Effective AI governance requires cross-functional collaboration.
  • AI governance addresses legal, ethical, operational, and reputational risks.
  • Maintaining regulatory alignment through compliance is crucial for AI governance.
  • Definition of an AI system (based on European Commission guidelines)
  • AI systems as machine-based systems with varying levels of autonomy and adaptiveness.
  • AI systems infer outputs based on input to generate predictions, recommendations, or decisions.
  • AI systems used in recruitment, customer service (chatbots), and medical diagnosis.
  • Human oversight of AI systems based on competence and authority.
  • Requirement for fundamental rights impact assessments for high-risk AI systems.
  • Obligations for AI deployers, including incident reporting and changes to AI system classification.
