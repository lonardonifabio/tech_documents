Mechanisms of AI Harm: Lessons Learned from AI Incidents

Authors: Mia Hofmann

This report, published by the Center for Security and Emerging Technology in October 2025, addresses the growing concern surrounding the potential harm caused by increasingly prevalent artificial intelligence systems, particularly generative models. The document highlights a surge in AI incident reports, indicating that existing harm prevention strategies are inadequate. It argues that a fundamental lack of understanding exists regarding how AI risks actually manifest in real-world scenarios. The core of the report centers on analyzing AI incident data from the AI Incident Database to identify six key mechanisms through which AI deployment leads to harm. These mechanisms, detailed in Table 1, are: Intentional Harm (including harm by design, AI misuse, and attacks on AI systems), Unintentional Harm (such as AI failures and failures of human oversight), and Integration Harm. The report emphasizes that a standardized, ‘one-size-fits-all’ approach to AI governance will be insufficient due to the diverse and complex pathways through which AI harm can occur. The analysis reveals that AI incidents are driven by a combination of deliberate malicious actions and unforeseen consequences arising from the technology's operation. The findings underscore the need for a more nuanced and adaptive approach to AI governance, one that acknowledges the multifaceted nature of AI risk and incorporates lessons learned from actual incidents. The document serves as a critical call to action for policymakers, developers, and users of AI to proactively address these emerging threats and ensure the responsible development and deployment of this transformative technology. Further research and data collection are strongly recommended to refine our understanding of these mechanisms and develop effective mitigation strategies. The report’s focus on incident analysis provides a practical framework for assessing and managing AI risks, moving beyond theoretical concerns to a grounded understanding of the challenges.

Key Concepts:

  • The report analyzes how AI deployment results in harm and identifies six key mechanisms that describe this process.
  • A one-size-fits-all approach to harm prevention will fall short due to the diverse pathways to AI harm.
  • Current approaches to harm prevention may be falling short as the number of AI harm incidents has surged.
  • Attacks on AI systems are driven by the intent to enable misuse.
  • Intentionality exists on a spectrum, ranging from truly unintended consequences to deliberate harm.
  • Lack of information regarding decision-making processes hinders the ability to determine whether harm was intentional or unintentional.
  • Building AI systems that are barely useful for non-malicious purposes due to misuse risks.
  • Attacks on AI systems originate from external attackers, not the developers themselves.
  • Cybersecurity attacks categorized into confidentiality, integrity, and availability attacks.
  • Algorithmic recommendations can create obstacles to human decision-making.
