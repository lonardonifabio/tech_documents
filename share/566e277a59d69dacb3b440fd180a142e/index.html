EU Funding and National Recovery Plans: Risks and Governance Challenges

Authors: Unknown, Unknown

This document highlights significant concerns regarding the governance and implementation of the EU's National Recovery and Resilience Plans (NRPs) and the broader framework for managing EU funds. A core issue identified is the prevalence of 'financing not linked to costs' models, which, as noted, prevented accurate measurement of EU fund efficiency and the establishment of actual costs associated with national plans. This approach, coupled with the reliance on milestone and target achievement for discharge decisions, creates substantial risk for Parliament, potentially requiring it to review massive expenditure volumes reported by Member States or the European Commission without sufficient scrutiny of underlying financial realities. The document emphasizes the potential for regional and local governments to be sidelined in the NRP preparation process, directly contradicting Article 6(2) of the draft NRP regulation which mandates their involvement. This exclusion represents a concerning trend within the EU's multi-level democratic structure, where the influence of central governments could overshadow the crucial contributions of regional and local authorities. Furthermore, the document criticizes the often-vague nature of newly proposed sectoral programmes, which frequently lack specific objectives and funding priorities, and the new Multi-Annual Financial Framework (MFF) governance rules. These rules introduce the possibility of significant financial envelope shifts without requiring legislative approval from the budgetary authority, increasing the risk of misuse and a lack of accountability. The absence of clearly defined delegated and implementing acts, in favor of basic acts, further exacerbates this concern. The overall implication is a system vulnerable to potential irregularities and lacking robust mechanisms for ensuring responsible and transparent utilization of EU funds, particularly given the large sums involved and the potential for significant impact on national economies and governance structures. The document underscores the need for stronger safeguards and a more participatory approach to ensure that EU funds are effectively deployed to achieve their intended goals while upholding principles of accountability and democratic oversight.
