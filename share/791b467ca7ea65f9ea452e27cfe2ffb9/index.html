AI in GovTech: what works, what fails, and why

Authors: Elena Sügis, Grace Smith, Bobby Chandan, Andres Raieste

This report, authored by Nortal experts, delves into the practical application of Artificial Intelligence (AI) within the public sector, specifically focusing on GovTech initiatives. It addresses the prevalent issue of overhyped AI tools and argues that the true value lies in strategically deploying AI solutions tailored to the unique needs of government organizations. The document outlines key considerations for successful AI implementation, including sector-driven capability building, AI-augmented government services, and the development of AI-ready digital public infrastructure. A significant portion of the report examines the common pitfalls that lead to AI projects stalling in the Proof of Concept (PoC) phase, highlighting challenges such as citizen adoption, unrealistic expectations regarding Return on Investment (ROI), budget constraints, and data-related hurdles including legal restrictions. The authors emphasize the importance of evaluating AI projects rigorously, determining when to pursue and when to pause initiatives. Furthermore, the report provides insights gleaned from successful AI deployments within government, offering a framework for key questions to ask and a realistic assessment of potential outcomes. It acknowledges the rapid pace of AI advancement and the need for governments to adapt proactively, urging a focus on building sustainable and impactful AI capabilities. The document concludes with a call for careful consideration of citizen adoption and the complexities surrounding data governance and legal compliance within the public sector. The report’s core message is that a thoughtful, needs-based approach to AI implementation is crucial for realizing its potential benefits in government.

Key Concepts:

  • The report focuses on understanding what works and what fails regarding AI implementation within government.
  • Successful AI deployment relies heavily on sector-driven capability building and aligning AI tools with specific public sector needs.
  • Many AI projects stall during the Proof of Concept (PoC) phase due to factors like citizen adoption challenges, budget constraints, and data-related restrictions.
  • AI projects often fail due to risks like hallucinations and misinformation.
  • Successful AI projects require alignment with strategic objectives and operational readiness.
  • Lack of clear leadership and ownership is a major obstacle to scaling AI projects beyond the PoC phase.
