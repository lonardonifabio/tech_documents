Digital Dependence of the Financial Sector: Risks, Resilience and European Autonomy

Authors: AFM, DNB

This document, titled "Digital Dependence of the Financial Sector: Risks, Resilience and European Autonomy," examines the increasing reliance of the financial sector on external IT service providers, particularly in the context of European autonomy. It highlights the significant risks associated with this dependency, focusing on the pervasive role of digital infrastructure and artificial intelligence (AI) within financial operations. The core argument centers around the fact that nearly all business processes – including customer engagement, risk management, compliance, and transaction processing – are now underpinned by digital systems. A key driver of this trend is the outsourcing of IT functions, encompassing cloud services, software vendors, and crucially, AI model providers. The document details the various risks stemming from this dependence, including vulnerabilities to disruptions, potential security breaches, and challenges to maintaining control over critical systems. It emphasizes the need for financial institutions and their suppliers to proactively manage these risks through awareness programs and mitigation strategies. The analysis includes a discussion of scenario analysis to prepare for disruptive events. Furthermore, the document explores the current legal and regulatory frameworks surrounding digital dependency, advocating for a European-wide approach to bolster resilience and strategic autonomy. It outlines how supervisory bodies like the AFM and DNB are addressing these concerns, focusing on requirements for risk management and supplier oversight. The document’s key message is that a proactive and coordinated European strategy is essential to safeguard the financial sector’s stability and independence in an increasingly digitalized world. The document's scope extends to the role of AI, recognizing its growing influence and the associated risks and opportunities. It suggests that strengthening strategic autonomy requires a collaborative European approach to address the vulnerabilities and ensure the long-term resilience of the financial sector.

Key Concepts:

  • The financial sector's increasing dependence on external IT service providers for core operations.
  • Risks associated with digital dependency, including reliance on IT infrastructure and the growing role of AI.
  • The need for a European approach to strengthening strategic autonomy and mitigating dependency risks through legislation and supervision.
  • The financial sector is driven by rapid technological innovation and the pursuit of economies of scale.
  • Digitisation of financial processes improves efficiency, speed, data quality, and enables real-time insight and automated reporting.
  • Increased reliance on IT introduces new vulnerabilities like cyber attacks and system failures, requiring stricter regulatory oversight.
  • The financial sector may encounter problems due to third-party dependencies, requiring institutions to maintain control over data access and jurisdiction.
  • Extreme, low-probability scenarios with high impact, such as geopolitical sanctions or hybrid attacks, can rapidly disrupt the financial sector.
  • Institutions can proactively strengthen preparedness through collaboration, joint exercises, and threat scenario development.
  • The need for regularly testing and revising contingency plans based on outcomes.
