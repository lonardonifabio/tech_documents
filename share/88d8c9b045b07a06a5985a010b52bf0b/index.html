Labeling of AI Agent Activity in Article 50 of the EU AI Act

Authors: Alan Chan

This Policy Brief, dated November 2025, focuses on the critical issue of labeling AI agent activity within the context of Article 50 of the EU AI Act. The document highlights the potential for AI agents to manipulate human beliefs and behaviors through synthetic online interactions, such as fabricated likes and shares on social media. It argues that transparent labeling of such activity as AI-generated is crucial for mitigating risks and fostering user awareness of influence attempts. Article 50 mandates that companies label specific AI activity, with enforcement expected to begin August 2, 2026. However, a key uncertainty remains: whether actions originating from AI agents will be subject to labeling requirements. The document proposes that a forthcoming Code of Practice (“Code”) presents an opportunity to clarify this ambiguity by outlining voluntary measures demonstrating compliance with Article 50. Specifically, it advocates for labeling web requests and browser actions initiated by AI agents, such as online payments. The brief emphasizes the need for proactive measures to ensure accountability and user protection in the face of increasingly sophisticated AI-driven influence operations. The document’s core argument centers on the likely interpretation of Article 50, suggesting a broad scope of activities involving AI agents should be subject to labeling to align with the Act’s objectives. The upcoming Code of Practice will be instrumental in shaping the practical application of this regulation and determining the specific criteria for demonstrating compliance.

Key Concepts:

  • AI agents distorting human beliefs and behaviors through synthetic interactions.
  • Labeling AI-generated activity as a method to mitigate risks and promote user awareness.
  • Requirement in the EU AI Act (Article 50) for companies to label certain AI activity, with potential clarification through a Code of Practice.
