EU AI Act: Best Practice for Compliance

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Stuart Macdonald, Andrea Frosinini, Stella Tsitsoula, Kinga Incze

This document, titled "EU AI Act: Best Practice for Compliance," outlines strategies for navigating the upcoming EU AI Act and ensuring responsible AI development and deployment. It emphasizes a shift from purely regulatory oversight to a measured approach, prioritizing transparency, safety, and innovation. Key recommendations include establishing clear documentation, audit trails, and traceable decision-making processes to classify AI risks and maintain compliance throughout the AI lifecycle. The document advocates for ‘Safety First’ principles, incorporating human oversight, robust ethical safeguards, and comprehensive data protection measures. Furthermore, it promotes ‘Innovation’ through continuous learning, retraining, and model refinement, alongside ‘Creative Experiments’ utilizing rapid testing and iterative improvements. A core theme is the recognition of AI's role in ‘Dual-Use Innovation,’ exemplified by the provided case studies. These examples highlight how core AI functionalities – sensing, positioning, computation, secure communications, and control – can be applied across both civilian and defense sectors. Specifically, the document details applications such as wind turbine blade manufacturing utilizing carbon fiber, drone airframes, high-powered lasers for communication and targeting, and encryption modules for both civilian and military applications. The ‘Technical Knowledge/Services’ section focuses on leveraging AI for GPS integration in civilian navigation systems and anti-jamming techniques for military navigation. The central concept is purpose-neutral functionality, where the same AI capabilities can be adapted for public benefit or, with appropriate safeguards, utilized in sensitive applications. The document’s aim is to provide practical guidance for organizations seeking to develop and deploy AI systems that align with the principles of the EU AI Act and foster responsible innovation within the field. It’s a forward-looking document, anticipating the regulatory landscape of 2026 and emphasizing the need for proactive compliance strategies.

Key Concepts:

  • Classifying AI risks and ensuring compliance at every stage.
  • Transparent Practices: Clear documentation, audit trails, and traceable decision-making.
  • Safety First: Human oversight, ethical safeguards, and robust data protection.
  • The report focuses on the regulatory implications of modifications to AI systems, particularly those involving GPAI models.
  • It emphasizes the assessment of substantial modifications, risk profiles, and provider obligations, rather than prescribing operational methods.
  • The report aims to equip organizations with a structured understanding of how modifications influence compliance responsibilities and supports informed decision-making.
  • Comprehensive documentation is resource-intensive due to parallel modifications.
  • Traceability is crucial when modifications impact system outputs with regulatory or societal implications.
  • Quantitative measurement of AI system modifications is challenging due to opacity, nonlinear effects, and limited access to baseline data.
