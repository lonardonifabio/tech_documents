EU AI Act Finland Country Profile

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Turo Manninen, Minna Saonegin, Andrey Prozorov, Paavo Ritala

This document, titled "EU AI Act Finland Country Profile," published in January 2026, provides an overview of Finland's preparations for the EU Artificial Intelligence Act. It highlights Finland's advancements in key areas crucial for responsible AI adoption, including data governance, high-performance computing, and the implementation of AI across sectors like health, mobility, and the bioeconomy. The profile specifically examines how Finnish organizations are utilizing AI-based solutions – such as those employed in recruitment, competence assessment, and employee experience management – often without explicitly recognizing them as "artificial intelligence," leading to what is termed "invisible AI." This phenomenon presents a significant challenge for regulation, as these systems subtly influence decision-making processes. The document identifies key challenges facing Finland, namely expanding supervisory capacity, clarifying requirements for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), strengthening conformity pathways, and ensuring transparency. Furthermore, it emphasizes the importance of investing in skills, fostering coordination, and establishing shared standards to guarantee that AI contributes to anticipatory, trustworthy, and resilient governance. The document’s authors represent a diverse range of expertise, including roles within information security, CGI, HR2Y OY, and LUT University, reflecting a multi-faceted approach to navigating the complexities of AI regulation and implementation. The core argument centers on the need for proactive measures to address the potential risks associated with ‘invisible AI’ and to ensure that Finland’s AI strategy aligns with the broader goals of the EU AI Act.

Key Concepts:

  • Finland is preparing for the EU Artificial Intelligence Act, focusing on data governance and responsible AI adoption.
  • The concept of 'invisible AI' refers to AI-based systems influencing decisions without explicit recognition as such.
  • Key challenges include expanding supervisory capacity, clarifying requirements for SMEs, and strengthening conformity pathways.
  • Applied research and commercial value creation through research breakthroughs.
  • Real-world experimentation and technology transfer via research centres and testbeds.
  • Government support for proof-of-concept and scale-up activities, particularly in generative AI for SMEs and mid-caps, alongside a focus on skills development and workforce adaptation.
