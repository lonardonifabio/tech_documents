EU AI Act & Korea AI Basic Act: A Mapping Exercise

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Kate Sotsenko, Jean NG, Yonah Welker, Robert A. Lienhard

This document, published in November 2025, presents a mapping exercise focusing on the emerging AI provider landscape in Korea in relation to key global regulations, specifically the EU AI Act and the Korea AI Basic Act. It details the nascent growth of Korea's AI provider market, noting initial activity starting in September 2024 with a single provider per month through April 2025. Despite the small absolute numbers, the document highlights the strategic importance of this development, emphasizing the potential for accelerated growth through strategic partnerships, targeted investment, and regulatory alignment. It argues that Korean AI development, often integrated into multinational platforms and exported globally, will likely be subject to compliance with regulations like the EU AI Act and U.S. regulations, impacting design, deployment, and risk management. The analysis considers the extraterritorial influence of Korean AI development, acknowledging its potential impact on markets like the United States, Europe, and Southeast Asia. The document implicitly suggests that proactive regulatory support and investment are crucial for Korea to establish a competitive position within the global AI ecosystem. Furthermore, it references OECD.AI data on model providers by country (October 2025) as a supporting indicator of this evolving landscape. The core argument centers on the need for Korea to proactively address regulatory compliance and foster a robust domestic AI ecosystem to capitalize on its emerging capabilities and influence within the global AI market. The document’s purpose is to provide a preliminary assessment and framework for understanding Korea’s position within the broader global AI governance and development context.

Key Concepts:

  • Korea's AI provider landscape is emerging and growing.
  • Strategic partnerships and investment can accelerate domestic AI capabilities.
  • Korean AI providers may be subject to compliance requirements under various regulations (EU AI Act, US regulations, and emerging Asian governance frameworks).
