Governing Large Language Models: A Cybernetic Approach to AI Compliance

Authors: Raimund Laqua, P.Eng., PMP

This briefing addresses the significant governance challenges posed by Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly their opacity and the virtually infinite range of their potential behaviors. It proposes a novel operational compliance framework for LLMs, rooted in cybernetic principles, specifically W. Ross Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety and Mick Ashby's work on ethical regulators. The core concept is a holistic model that integrates governance as a fundamental layer, rather than a supplementary control. This architecture incorporates three levels of regulation: transaction controls focused on individual interactions, adaptive controls designed to maintain the effectiveness of the transaction controls, and a governance layer responsible for making value judgments. The document emphasizes that effective LLM regulation necessitates controlling inputs, model selection, and outputs, acknowledging the inherent complexity of the transformation process between these elements – essentially accepting the ‘black box’ nature of LLM operation. Critically, it highlights that static controls are inadequate against high-variety systems like LLMs. Therefore, the operational compliance architecture must be capable of learning and adapting, mirroring Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety. The framework aims to provide a practical approach to managing the risks and complexities associated with deploying and utilizing LLMs, focusing on a dynamic and responsive system rather than rigid, predefined rules. The briefing suggests a layered approach, recognizing the limitations of direct control and advocating for a system that can continuously adjust to the evolving behavior of the model. This proactive strategy is essential for ensuring compliance and responsible AI development in the face of the rapidly advancing capabilities of Large Language Models.

Key Concepts:

  • The challenge of regulating opaque and highly variable Large Language Models.
  • A cybernetic approach to AI compliance utilizing the Law of Requisite Variety for adaptive controls.
  • A holistic operational compliance model encompassing governance as an integral layer, including transaction, adaptive, and governance controls.
  • Third-order regulation is ethical regulation and a governance layer within operational compliance.
  • Governance is integrated within operational compliance, not external to it, and requires rigorous documentation and justification.
  • Continuous improvement applies to governance, requiring examination of criteria for evolving purposes and contexts.
