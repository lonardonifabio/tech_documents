Data Act versus EU AI Act: A Mapping Exercise

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Richard Self, Lord Chris Holmes

This report, published by AI & Partners, provides a mapping exercise comparing the Data Act and the EU AI Act. AI & Partners, a non-profit organization, focuses on defending digital rights and promoting responsible AI development. The organization’s mission is to mitigate the risks associated with uncontrolled AI adoption, which can lead to confusion, reputational damage, and loss of control. They achieve this by offering tools and services to help organizations identify, monitor, and govern their AI systems, ensuring responsible AI usage for both the organization and its clients. The report highlights the importance of proactive governance and risk management within the evolving landscape of AI regulations, particularly in the context of the EU AI Act and the Data Act. AI & Partners advocates for a future where AI unlocks potential rather than causing harm, emphasizing the need for trust and accountability in AI development and deployment. The organization's work involves direct technical support, policy engagement, global advocacy, and participation in industry groups like AI Commons, demonstrating a multi-faceted approach to safeguarding digital rights and promoting responsible AI practices. The report underscores the critical role of organizations like AI & Partners in navigating the complexities of emerging AI regulations and fostering a more secure and trustworthy AI ecosystem.

Key Concepts:

  • AI should unlock potential, not cause harm.
  • Safe AI usage for organizations and clients.
  • Tools to identify, monitor, and govern AI systems.
  • Public sector data requests under the Data Act are permitted for public emergencies or legal tasks, subject to justification and limitations.
  • The Data Act protects sensitive data, including personal information and trade secrets, ensuring it remains non-publicly available.
  • Dispute resolution mechanisms, including voluntary settlement bodies, are established to address disagreements related to data access and protection, with courts remaining as a final recourse.
  • Seamless interoperability between data spaces and data processing services
  • Harmonizing technical standards to support innovation and cross-border data use
  • Avoiding vendor lock-in and simplifying switching between cloud providers
