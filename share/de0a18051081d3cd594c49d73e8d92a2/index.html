Foundation Model Transparency Index Results

Authors: Unknown

This document presents the findings of the 2023 Foundation Model Transparency Index, assessing the transparency practices of leading AI development companies. The overall results reveal a concerning lack of transparency, with an average score of only 37 out of 100, highlighting that most companies struggle to provide sufficient detail about their model development processes. A significant degree of heterogeneity exists, as 82 out of 100 indicators were evaluated by at least one company, indicating varying levels of scrutiny and disclosure. The study observed a downward trend in scores from 2024 to 2025, with Amazon, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Mistral, and OpenAI experiencing declines, while AI21 Labs saw a substantial drop. Notably, despite some companies like AI21 Labs disclosing substantial information in 2024 (including training compute, energy usage, and carbon emissions), this disclosure ceased in 2025. Developers were categorized into three groups: four companies (Meta, Hugging Face, OpenAI, Stability AI) consistently scored above the mean, three (Google, Anthropic, Cohere) around the mean, and three (AI21 Labs, Inflection, Amazon) significantly below. The document emphasizes that nearly all companies exhibited opacity regarding critical aspects such as training data, data labor, computational costs, risk mitigation strategies, feedback mechanisms, and the potential downstream impacts of their models. The findings underscore the need for greater transparency in the AI industry to foster accountability, trust, and responsible development. The index's publication garnered media attention, as evidenced by reports by Roose (2023) and Hao (2023), and was integrated into further research and analysis. The analysis suggests a critical gap in the industry's commitment to open and transparent practices, particularly concerning the significant resources and potential impacts associated with foundation model development.
