Official Artificial Intelligence Impact Assessment Tool

Authors: Digital Transformation Agency (DTA)

This document provides an official Artificial Intelligence impact assessment tool developed by the Digital Transformation Agency (DTA) of Australia. Its primary purpose is to assist Australian Government agencies in evaluating their proposed implementations of Artificial Intelligence (AI). The tool is offered alongside supporting guidance materials to facilitate a thorough assessment process. Crucially, the document explicitly states that the tool should not be considered legal advice and does not authorize any specific AI use. Agencies retain full responsibility for all decisions related to their AI deployments and are strongly advised to seek independent technical and legal counsel as needed. The DTA encourages feedback and suggestions regarding the tool and its associated guidance, directing inquiries to ai@dta.gov.au. The document acknowledges the DTA's efforts to ensure the accuracy of the information presented, but importantly, it disclaims any guarantee of complete accuracy or comprehensiveness, advising against relying solely on this information for commercial decisions. The DTA is dedicated to creating accessible web content, and users experiencing difficulties accessing the document are encouraged to contact ai@dta.gov.au for assistance. Furthermore, the document outlines its maintenance by the DTA and its role in supporting the Australian Government's Policy for the responsible use of AI. The Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International Licence applies to the content, allowing for reuse with appropriate attribution. The DTA's commitment to accessibility is highlighted, and alternative contact information is provided for accessibility-related inquiries.

Key Concepts:

  • The DTA provides an impact assessment tool to Australian Government agencies for evaluating AI use.
  • Agencies are responsible for AI decisions and should seek appropriate advice.
  • The tool is not legal advice and accuracy is not guaranteed, so reliance should not be solely on it for commercial decisions.
  • Planned transparency measures to communicate information about AI use cases.
  • Identifying and consulting relevant stakeholders for AI systems.
  • Human oversight and validation of AI recommendations and decisions, and their influence on human decision-making.
  • The assessment reflects the AI use case's scope, function, and current controls.
  • Risk likelihood scale defines probabilities for various outcomes (Almost certain, Likely, Possible, Unlikely, Rare).
  • Risk matrix categorizes risks based on likelihood and impact (Insignificant, Minor, Moderate, Major, Severe).
