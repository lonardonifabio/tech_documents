Detecting the Unknown: A Guide to Threat Hunting v2.0

Authors: National Cyber Security Strategy Team, Cabinet Office

This document, "Detecting the Unknown: A Guide to Threat Hunting v2.0," outlines the importance of proactive threat hunting within UK government departments. It references the National Cyber Security Strategy 2016-2021, which aims for the UK to be secure and resilient against cyber threats. The strategy emphasizes the need for departments to move beyond simply meeting the Minimum Cyber Security Standard (MCSS) established in June 2018. Instead, it advocates for investing in the development and mobilization of Threat Hunting capabilities – a proactive, iterative, and human-centric approach to identifying cyber threats that have bypassed traditional security controls. Threat Hunting is defined as Incident Response without the Incident, focusing on internal threats. The document stresses that establishing a robust Threat Hunting function will enhance a department’s security posture, allowing for earlier detection of malicious activity and minimizing the potential damage from cyberattacks. Key enablers for a successful Threat Hunting capability include Cyber Threat Intelligence, relevant data from across the IT estate, and appropriate investment in skilled personnel. The overall goal is to improve resilience and reduce risk by actively seeking out and neutralizing threats before they can cause significant harm. The document highlights the strategic alignment between Threat Hunting and the broader National Cyber Security Strategy.

Key Concepts:

  • Threat Hunting is a proactive, iterative, and human-centric approach to identifying cyber threats.
  • Departments should invest in developing Threat Hunting capabilities to improve security posture and reduce risk.
  • Threat Hunting aims to identify malicious activity early in an attack to minimize damage.
  • Proactive Security vs. Reactive Monitoring
  • Threat Hunting Process: Hypothesis Generation and Testing
  • Iteration of Threat Hunting: Testing and Remediation
  • Threat Hunting: The process of proactively searching for threats within an organization's systems.
  • CIA Triad: A foundational model for security – Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability.
  • Cognitive Bias: Systematic patterns of deviation from norm or rationality in judgment, impacting decision-making.
  • DFIR Skills: Digital Forensic and Incident Response skills, including endpoint forensics, network forensics, and malware analysis for complex investigations.
