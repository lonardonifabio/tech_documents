Guidance on AI and Children 3.0

Authors: UNICEF

This document, titled "Guidance on AI and Children 3.0," released in December 2025, provides updated recommendations for governments and businesses to develop AI policies and systems that prioritize and protect children's rights. The core of the guidance focuses on establishing a ‘child-centred AI’ approach, recognizing the significant and rapidly increasing influence of AI in children’s lives. It outlines ten key requirements for such systems, including ensuring regulatory frameworks, prioritizing child safety, safeguarding data privacy, promoting non-discrimination and fairness, demanding transparency and accountability, and upholding human and child rights. Specifically, sections detail how to address these requirements through measures like oversight, compliance, safety protocols, data protection strategies, and mechanisms for ensuring fairness and preventing bias. The document emphasizes the importance of providing transparency, explainability, and accountability to children regarding AI systems. Furthermore, it advocates for respecting children’s best interests, fostering inclusion, and preparing children for the future developments in AI. The guidance also includes cross-cutting recommendations and considerations, highlighting the need for an enabling environment for child-centred AI. The document is supported by data illustrating the growing adoption of AI among children, citing statistics from the UK, the US, and Argentina, demonstrating the urgency of addressing the ethical and developmental implications of AI’s increasing presence in children’s digital experiences. The document’s purpose is to guide the creation of AI systems that are safe, equitable, and beneficial for children, ultimately contributing to their well-being and development in an increasingly AI-driven world. It’s a proactive approach to mitigate potential risks and maximize the positive opportunities presented by AI for younger generations. The document’s forward-looking perspective anticipates the ongoing evolution of AI and emphasizes the need for continuous adaptation and refinement of policies and practices.

Key Concepts:

  • Development of AI policies and systems to uphold children’s rights.
  • Ensuring safety and protection of children’s data and privacy in the context of AI.
  • Promoting non-discrimination, fairness, and accountability in AI systems used by children.
  • AI systems infer outputs based on objectives and input data.
  • AI systems can be autonomous and adaptive, utilizing methods like explicit rules, supervised/unsupervised learning, or reinforcement learning.
  • The need for digital governance frameworks to address AI's impact on children and the broader international community.
  • Government AI systems must comply with AI-related laws and child rights law.
  • Capacity-building on AI and child rights is critical for State actors to understand risks and opportunities.
  • Child rights impact assessments are a prerequisite for public procurement and deployment of AI tools, with findings needing to be publicly available.
