AI Agents and Global Governance: Analyzing Foundational Legal, Policy, and Accountability Tools

Authors: Talita Dias

This document explores the critical challenges and opportunities presented by the rise of AI agents, particularly concerning global governance and accountability. It begins by examining cross-border harms potentially caused by autonomous AI agents, focusing on the limitations of existing international law and the need for non-binding global norms to address these risks. The analysis delves into the development of global accountability mechanisms, recognizing their current shortcomings. A significant portion of the document is dedicated to the human rights impacts of AI agents, highlighting the potential for unintended consequences and the necessity for proactive safeguards. The research investigates how these agents, capable of independent action and interaction with external systems, could exacerbate existing AI risks and introduce new ones due to their ability to directly influence software and hardware. The document emphasizes the importance of a comprehensive approach, combining legal frameworks, policy guidelines, and robust accountability measures to manage the evolving landscape of AI agents. It identifies gaps in current strategies and proposes recommendations for addressing the complex challenges posed by this rapidly advancing technology. The core argument centers on the need for international collaboration and the development of adaptable governance structures to ensure that AI agents are deployed responsibly and ethically, minimizing potential harm and maximizing their benefits.

Key Concepts:

  • AI agents can set their own goals and make plans with a high degree of autonomy.
  • The potential for AI agents to interact with external systems and affect software/hardware raises new risks.
  • The need for global accountability mechanisms to address harms caused by AI agents.
  • Lack of universally binding AI-specific international rules.
  • Need for inclusive global governance of AI.
  • Importance of leveraging existing foundational global governance tools.
  • Risk of new rules undermining established protections.
  • States' obligation to exercise due diligence in their territory.
  • Application of due diligence to prevent cross-border harms regardless of source.
  • Due diligence obligations in the context of AI agents and environmental harm.
