Made in Europe EU AI Act Prohibited AI Practices : A Primer

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Hr Dr. Takahisa Karita, Saeed Al Dhaheri, Agata Wolska, Cristiana Vergara

This primer, released in November 2025, focuses on the implications of the EU AI Act, specifically Article 5 which prohibits ‘prohibited AI practices.’ The document highlights elevated risks for high-algorithmic management countries under this article, urging proactive compliance. A key recommendation is to restrict the use of emotion inference AI within workplaces and educational institutions. It references the European Parliament’s report, ‘AI Act: Digitalisation, artificial intelligence and algorithmic management in the workplace: Shaping the future of work’ (October 2025), which explores the broader impact of AI on the workforce. The excerpt presents data from various surveys including EWCS 2024, EB2024, ESENER 2024, and OECD 2024, detailing the prevalence of advanced management (AM) practices and the deployment of AI-enabled systems in workplaces. Specifically, the EWCS and EB surveys indicate that over 50% of workers in countries such as Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Ireland, and the Netherlands are exposed to AM practices, while ESENER reports a lower, 10-20% deployment rate among companies. The data further illustrates the varying levels of AI adoption across different European nations, providing a snapshot of the current landscape. The document emphasizes the need for organizations to understand and address the potential risks associated with AI systems, particularly those involving emotion inference, in light of the stringent regulations outlined in the EU AI Act. The data presented suggests a significant portion of the workforce is already impacted by algorithmic management, necessitating careful consideration and mitigation strategies. The document serves as a preliminary guide for navigating the complexities of the EU AI Act and its impact on businesses and workers.

Key Concepts:

  • Prohibited AI Practices under Article 5 of the EU AI Act, specifically restricting emotion-inference AI in workplaces and educational institutions.
  • High-algorithmic management countries face elevated EU AI Act risks.
  • Over 50% of workers in certain countries are exposed to advanced management practices, while a smaller percentage of companies deploy AI-enabled systems.
  • Indiscriminate collection of facial images for facial recognition databases
  • Violation of privacy and data protection laws due to lack of consent and control over data
  • AI's limited role in predicting criminal behavior based solely on profiling, but potential support for human decision-making with objective data and oversight
