Multinational Advanced AI Development: Costs and Strategic Implications

Authors: Pilz, Cottier

This document analyzes the substantial financial barriers associated with developing and maintaining 'frontier' AI models, particularly focusing on the challenges faced by individual nations and the potential benefits of collaborative efforts. It highlights that the projected costs of training state-of-the-art AI models, potentially exceeding several billions per model by 2028, are simply unsustainable for most countries operating under conventional economic models. The document emphasizes that the required infrastructure investments – tens of billions of dollars – necessitate significant datacenter capacity and continuous research and development (R&D) to maintain a competitive edge. Specifically, the costs associated with maintaining a frontier AI program, including the ongoing need for compute power and model updates, are projected to be in the range of $0.5B to $25B by 2028. The analysis contrasts these costs with the scale of initiatives like OpenAI’s Stargate project, which is estimated to exceed $100 billion annually. The document suggests that a collective approach, involving a group of ‘bridge powers’ – nations capable of pooling resources – is crucial to achieving a level of AI capability and competitiveness that would be unattainable for any single entity. The figure 2 illustrates the projected hardware and training costs, showing a significant increase over the 2025-2028 period. The document references sources including Pilz et al., 2025; Cottier et al., 2024; and Epoch AI, 2025, and mentions specific European supercomputers like Jupiter (Germany) and Alice Recoque (France) as examples of the infrastructure being developed. The core argument is that without a coordinated, multinational strategy, nations risk falling behind in the rapidly evolving field of advanced AI. The need for politically untenable costs associated with a war-time economy is also highlighted as a potential consequence of attempting to sustain a frontier AI program alone.

Key Concepts:

  • Blueprint for Multinational Advanced AI Development
  • Collaboration between leading AI researchers and institutions
  • Governance and safety considerations for AI development
  • High, fixed costs of training frontier models necessitate collaboration.
  • Groups of 'bridge powers' can achieve greater capability through collective investment in AI infrastructure.
  • Maintaining frontier AI competitiveness requires continuous research and development, leading to substantial and potentially unsustainable costs for individual nations.
  • Nations will need to take rapid action to seize economic, security, and sovereignty benefits.
  • Latecomers to AI development risk lasting strategic weakness due to increasing barriers to entry.
  • A coordinated bridge powers can afford current frontier training costs, but non-frontier states face systematic erosion of economic competitiveness if AI concentrates economic value in frontier-controlling states.
  • Multinational development can structurally incentivize broader technology diffusion compared to monopolistic control.
