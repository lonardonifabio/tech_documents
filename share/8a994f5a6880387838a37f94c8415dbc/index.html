CSA Zero Trust Research Working Group - ZT7 Pillar: Data

Authors: Shruti Kulkarni, Michael Roza

This document, produced by the Cloud Security Alliance (CSA) Zero Trust Research Working Group, focuses on the ZT7 pillar: Data. The core objectives of the working group are to collaboratively develop and promote Zero Trust best practices, recognizing its relevance across diverse environments including cloud, on-premises, mobile endpoints, IoT, and operational technology (OT). A key element of their approach is to provide thought leadership and educate the industry on the various strengths and weaknesses of different Zero Trust methodologies, empowering organizations to make informed decisions aligned with their specific business requirements, risk profiles, and strategic priorities. The group advocates for a deliberate, product and vendor-neutral approach to Zero Trust architecture and implementation, ensuring a mature and robust security posture. Specifically, the document emphasizes taking a technically sound position on Zero Trust principles and offering defensible recommendations while maintaining neutrality regarding specific products or vendors. The ZT7 pillar: Data, led by Shruti Kulkarni and Krishna Narayanaswamy, is central to this effort. The CSA’s goal is to foster a comprehensive understanding of Zero Trust within the broader security landscape, ultimately contributing to more resilient and adaptable security strategies. The document’s creation reflects the CSA’s commitment to driving innovation and best practices in cloud and cybersecurity.

Key Concepts:

  • Zero Trust as a modern necessity and Hybrid/Cloud approach to improving Information Security.
  • Product and vendor-neutral approach to Zero Trust architectures and implementation.
  • Defensible recommendations for Zero Trust implementation based on business needs and risks.
  • Standardized log formats are crucial for aggregating and analyzing data from diverse sources in a Zero Trust architecture.
  • Consistent log formats, such as UTC-based timestamps, enable accurate event correlation and reduce ambiguity.
  • Diverse log sources (firewalls, endpoints, cloud services, applications) contribute critical data for security analysis and investigation.
  • The principle of logging as a source of information within a Zero Trust framework.
  • The importance of filtering and managing log velocity to focus on critical events.
  • Utilizing aggregated logs for identifying policy failures, authentication issues, and attacks.
  • Integrating Visibility and Analytics with Automation and Orchestration for a closed-loop system.
