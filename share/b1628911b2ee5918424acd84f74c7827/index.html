Intolerable Risk Threshold Recommendations for Artificial Intelligence

Authors: Deepika Raman, Nada Madkour, Evan R. Murphy, Krystal Jackson, Jessica Newman

This document, produced by the UC Berkeley Center for Long-Term Cybersecurity, presents key principles, considerations, and case studies aimed at informing the development of robust AI safety frameworks for both industry and government. The central focus is on establishing 'intolerable risk thresholds' for artificial intelligence, a critical area of concern as AI systems become increasingly powerful and potentially disruptive. The paper outlines a framework for defining these thresholds, recognizing the complex challenges posed by frontier AI. It details the rationale behind the need for such thresholds, acknowledging the potential for unintended consequences and the importance of proactive risk management. The document explores various considerations, including ethical implications, societal impacts, and technical limitations. It provides a structured approach for organizations and policymakers to assess and mitigate risks associated with advanced AI. The authors emphasize the need for a collaborative effort involving diverse stakeholders to ensure responsible AI development and deployment. The paper’s findings are intended to contribute to the ongoing discourse surrounding AI safety and guide the creation of effective regulatory and governance mechanisms. It’s a timely contribution given the rapid advancements in AI and the growing urgency to address potential risks. The document’s scope is broad, encompassing a range of perspectives and potential applications. The authors clearly state that the views expressed are their own and do not represent the views of UC Berkeley or other affiliated institutions, highlighting the independent nature of the research.
