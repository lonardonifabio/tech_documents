Germany Country Profile: Preparing for the EU AI Act

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Markus Krebsz, Michael Berns, Mike Flache, Sandro Mendonca

This country profile, dated January 2026, focuses on Germany's preparations for the implementation of the EU Artificial Intelligence Act. It details the nation's advancements in key areas crucial for AI deployment, including data governance, high-performance computing, and responsible AI adoption across vital sectors like healthcare, mobility, and biotechnology. The document specifically highlights Germany's efforts to align with the regulatory framework established by the EU AI Act, emphasizing the need to address ongoing challenges. These challenges encompass expanding supervisory capabilities to effectively oversee AI systems, clarifying specific requirements for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) to ensure compliance, and strengthening conformity pathways to streamline the process of bringing AI solutions to market. Furthermore, the profile underscores the importance of transparency in AI development and deployment. Germany's strategy involves substantial investment in developing a skilled workforce, fostering effective coordination among stakeholders, and establishing shared standards to promote interoperability and trust. The report suggests that by prioritizing these investments, Germany can leverage AI to create anticipatory, trustworthy, and resilient governance structures. The document is authored by a team of experts including Markus Krebsz, the creator of the Enterprise-wide AI Risk Management (EW-AiRM) framework, Michael Berns from PwC Deutschland, Mike Flache from the Digital Growth Collective, and Sandro Mendonca from the European Patent Office, indicating a multi-faceted approach to AI governance and risk management. The Executive Summary reiterates the core findings, emphasizing Germany's proactive steps towards aligning with the EU's AI regulations and the strategic importance of addressing the identified challenges for sustained and responsible AI development.

Key Concepts:

  • Germany's preparation for the EU AI Act and its impact on regulation and capability.
  • Progress in data governance, high-performance computing, and responsible AI adoption across sectors like health, mobility, and biotechnology.
  • Key challenges including supervisory capacity, SME requirements, conformity pathways, and ensuring transparency in AI development and deployment.
