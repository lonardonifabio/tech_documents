Serious Incident Reporting: Ensuring Public Safety

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Félicien Vallet, Shraeyas Jayasimha R, Juhi Koré, Jeremy Prasetyo EU

This document, titled "Serious Incident Reporting: Ensuring Public Safety," analyzes emerging trends in AI incident reporting, primarily focusing on data collected from May – September 2025. The core finding is that accountability and transparency are the most frequently reported areas within AI incidents, representing 336 reports in September 2025. Transparency, safety, and robustness also receive significant attention, though recent declines may indicate improvements in system resilience or changes in reporting criteria. Notably, concerns regarding fairness, human wellbeing, and sustainability consistently lag behind, suggesting potential blind spots in risk assessment. The report emphasizes the need for more comprehensive AI risk management frameworks that move beyond traditional governance and safety considerations to encompass broader social, ethical, and environmental impacts. The analysis draws parallels with data related to the OECD AI: Evolution of Incidents and Hazards by AI Principle. It argues for proactive reporting frameworks that address the full spectrum of AI risks, rather than solely focusing on compliance with regulations like the EU AI Act (Article 73). The document highlights the importance of a holistic approach, recognizing that AI incidents can manifest in diverse ways and require a multi-faceted understanding to effectively mitigate potential harm. The data suggests a shift towards more robust systems and potentially narrower reporting criteria, but also underscores the critical need to address underreported risks related to human wellbeing and sustainability. Ultimately, the report advocates for a more balanced and forward-looking approach to AI incident monitoring and reporting.

Key Concepts:

  • Evolving AI incident monitoring priorities
  • Accountability as the most frequently reported AI incident area
  • Potential blind spots in AI risk assessment (fairness, wellbeing, sustainability)
  • Need for balanced and proactive reporting frameworks encompassing social, ethical, and environmental dimensions of AI impact
  • Responsibility for reporting serious incidents lies with the AI system provider.
  • Deployer may have obligations depending on the incident and contractual arrangements.
  • Timeframes for reporting incidents (15 days, 2 days, 10 days) based on severity and circumstances.
  • Public Trust in Technology
  • Responsible AI Practices
  • Human Rights Harm (violation of fundamental rights due to AI)
