AI Innovations in Public Services: The Case of National Libraries

Authors: Ines Mergel, Carsten Schmidt

This book, "AI Innovations in Public Services: The Case of National Libraries," explores the burgeoning integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies within the operations and strategic frameworks of national libraries worldwide. It delves into the diverse applications of AI, ranging from automated cataloging and digitization of historical collections to personalized user experiences and enhanced research support. The book examines how national libraries are leveraging AI to address critical challenges such as preservation of cultural heritage, accessibility of information, and evolving user needs in the digital age. Specifically, the work investigates the practical implementation of AI solutions across various library functions. This includes examining the use of machine learning algorithms for metadata extraction and classification, natural language processing (NLP) for text analysis and information retrieval, and computer vision techniques for image recognition and optical character recognition (OCR) – all crucial for managing and accessing vast archives. The authors analyze case studies from national libraries across different countries, highlighting both successes and challenges encountered during the adoption of AI. Furthermore, the book addresses the ethical considerations surrounding AI deployment in cultural institutions, including issues of bias in algorithms, data privacy, and the potential impact on human roles within the library environment. It emphasizes the importance of responsible AI development and implementation to ensure that these technologies serve to enhance, rather than diminish, the core values of national libraries – namely, access to knowledge, preservation of cultural heritage, and community engagement. The research considers the broader implications of AI for the future of libraries, including the potential for new service models, collaborative partnerships, and innovative approaches to knowledge management. The book provides a valuable resource for librarians, information scientists, AI researchers, and policymakers interested in understanding and shaping the role of AI in the evolving landscape of public services and cultural institutions. It offers a nuanced perspective on how national libraries can harness the power of AI to remain relevant and impactful in the 21st century.

Key Concepts:
