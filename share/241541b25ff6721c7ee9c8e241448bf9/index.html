The Human Advantage: Stronger Brains in the Age of AI

This World Economic Forum Insight Report, published in January 2026, explores the critical role of human brain health and capabilities in an era increasingly shaped by artificial intelligence. The report, a collaborative effort with the McKinsey Health Institute, argues against a simplistic ‘humans vs. AI’ narrative, instead advocating for a synergistic approach where human strengths are amplified by AI. It identifies key areas for focus: safeguarding brain health, fostering brain skills, studying brain capital, investing in brain capital, and mobilizing for brain capital. Specifically, the document highlights the need to proactively manage cognitive decline, cultivate uniquely human skills like creativity and critical thinking, and recognize the value of ‘brain capital’ – encompassing cognitive abilities and their potential – as a key differentiator in the future workforce. The report emphasizes that the future isn't about replacing human intelligence with machines, but rather about leveraging AI to unlock and enhance human potential. It suggests a strategic investment in initiatives that promote cognitive well-being and develop skills that complement AI's capabilities. The document’s findings are presented as a contribution to a broader World Economic Forum project and do not necessarily represent the views of the entire organization or its stakeholders. The report’s structure includes a foreword, executive summary, introduction, and detailed sections outlining specific recommendations and strategies for maximizing human advantage in the age of AI. It concludes with contributors and endnotes, providing further context and supporting information. The core message is that human intelligence, when strategically nurtured and combined with AI, holds the key to continued innovation and progress.

Key Concepts:

  • Artificial intelligence continues to transform the way we live and work, raising questions about the future of human capabilities.
  • The future is about collaboration between humans and AI, focusing on maximizing potential.
  • Investing in brain capital is crucial for navigating the age of AI.
  • Strong brain health is crucial for resilient individuals and communities.
  • Underinvestment in brain health results in significant opportunity costs and increased disease burden.
  • Brain health conditions, particularly neurodegenerative diseases, are a growing global concern, impacting a large population and disrupting lives.
  • Investing in brain skills early yields significant returns, particularly through quality early childhood programs.
  • The evolving skill demands of employers, as identified by the World Economic Forum, necessitate substantial training for a large percentage of the workforce.
  • ‘Brain skills’ – encompassing analytical thinking, resilience, creativity, and technological literacy – are consistently prioritized by employers as critical for both current and future workplace needs.
  • The framework enables tracking global investment and returns related to brain capital.
