The Economics of Copyright and AI: Empirical Evidence and Optimal Policy

Authors: Christian Peukert

This in-depth analysis, commissioned by the European Parliament’s Policy Department for Justice, Civil Liberties and Institutional Affairs, addresses the critical intersection of copyright policy and the rapidly evolving landscape of artificial intelligence (AI). The document, requested by the Committee on Legal Affairs, undertakes a comprehensive examination of how copyright frameworks should adapt to the challenges and opportunities presented by AI. It leverages historical insights from digital markets, a nuanced understanding of the economic value of data, and a formal economic model to rigorously assess the welfare implications of various policy options. Specifically, the analysis evaluates several potential approaches to copyright licensing in the context of AI, including a standard exception, an exception with an opt-out mechanism, a licensing market operating on an opt-out basis, and statutory licensing. The core objective is to identify the most effective policy solution that maximizes societal welfare while safeguarding the rights of copyright holders. The study incorporates empirical evidence to ground its analysis and employs a formal model to quantify the potential economic consequences of each policy alternative. The document’s findings are intended to inform the European Parliament’s deliberations on this complex issue, providing a robust and evidence-based framework for shaping copyright policy in the age of AI. It highlights the need for a balanced approach that fosters innovation in AI while ensuring that creators are appropriately compensated for their work. Furthermore, the analysis considers the potential impact of different licensing models on market competition and the overall flow of information. The research delves into the complexities of data ownership and usage rights, recognizing that data is a crucial input for AI development. The document’s conclusions will be valuable to policymakers, legal scholars, and industry stakeholders involved in shaping the future of copyright and AI. The study’s methodology and findings contribute to a deeper understanding of the economic dynamics at play and offer a pathway towards a more sustainable and equitable system.
