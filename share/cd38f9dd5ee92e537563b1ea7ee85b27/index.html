The Human Advantage: Stronger Brains in the Age of AI

This report, published by the World Economic Forum in January 2026, explores the evolving relationship between humans and artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the enduring ‘human advantage’ in an age increasingly shaped by AI. The document argues against a simplistic view of humans versus machines, instead advocating for a collaborative approach where human capabilities are leveraged alongside AI to unlock new levels of creativity, productivity, and overall impact. The core of the report centers around the concept of ‘brain capital’ – referring to the cognitive abilities, skills, and knowledge of individuals – and proposes strategies for safeguarding, fostering, studying, and investing in this capital. It outlines five key areas for action: 1) Safeguarding brain health through preventative measures and lifestyle choices, 2) Fostering brain skills – emphasizing adaptability, critical thinking, and learning agility – 3) Studying brain capital to understand its value and potential, 4) Investing in brain capital through education, training, and development programs, and 5) Mobilizing for brain capital to translate knowledge and skills into tangible outcomes. The report highlights the importance of recognizing that while AI can automate tasks and augment capabilities, uniquely human traits like creativity, emotional intelligence, and complex problem-solving will remain crucial. It’s a forward-looking analysis designed to guide strategic thinking for individuals, organizations, and policymakers navigating the transformative impact of AI. The document emphasizes a proactive stance, urging stakeholders to prioritize human cognitive development and investment to ensure continued relevance and success in a world dominated by intelligent machines. The World Economic Forum’s collaboration with the McKinsey Health Institute underscores the significance of this topic within the broader context of global health and well-being. The report’s findings are presented as a contribution to a larger project, acknowledging that they represent a collaborative effort and do not necessarily reflect the entirety of the World Economic Forum’s perspectives.

Key Concepts:

  • Artificial intelligence continues to transform the way we live and work, raising questions about the future of human capabilities.
  • The future is about collaboration between humans and AI to unlock new levels of creativity and productivity.
  • Investing in brain capital is crucial for individuals and society in the age of AI.
  • Strong brain health is crucial for resilient individuals and communities.
  • Underinvestment in brain health results in significant opportunity costs and increased disease burden.
  • Brain health conditions, particularly neurodegenerative disorders like Alzheimer’s, are a growing global concern, impacting millions of lives.
  • Investing in brain skills early yields significant returns, particularly through quality early childhood programs.
  • The evolving skill demands of employers, as identified by the World Economic Forum, necessitate substantial training for a large percentage of the workforce.
  • ‘Brain skills’ – encompassing analytical thinking, resilience, creativity, and technological literacy – are consistently prioritized by employers as critical for both current and future workplace needs.
  • The framework enables tracking global investment and returns related to brain capital.
