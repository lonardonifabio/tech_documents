Global Catastrophic Risks 2026

Authors: Fatima Denton, Johan Rockström, Manjana Milkoreit, David Obura, Wilfred Wan

The "Global Catastrophic Risks 2026" report, published by the Global Challenges Foundation in 2026, examines potential global catastrophic risks, focusing on Earth system stability and the implications of various escalating threats. The report identifies four key risks, each analyzed through the lens of expert perspectives from leading organizations and academic institutions. Risk 1 investigates the concept of Earth system tipping points and the associated responsibility for mitigating potential disasters, featuring contributions from the UNU-INRA and the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research. Risk 2 explores the challenges of global governance concerning nature and biodiversity, drawing on the expertise of IPBES and CORDIO East Africa. Risk 3 addresses the escalating risks posed by Weapons of Mass Destruction and multi-domain escalation, informed by the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute's (SIPRI) Weapons of Mass Destruction Programme. Finally, Risk 4 delves into the increasingly concerning role of Artificial Intelligence (AI) within military decision-making processes, acknowledging the potential for amplified risks. The report emphasizes the need for imaginative thinking and proactive responsibility in addressing these complex and interconnected challenges. It highlights the importance of collaboration across disciplines and international organizations to prevent potentially irreversible consequences. The document is a foundational report outlining critical global risks, utilizing a multidisciplinary approach to assess and understand the potential for catastrophic events. The report’s creation involved a collaborative effort, bringing together researchers, directors, and experts from diverse fields, reflecting a holistic approach to global risk assessment. The inclusion of figures like Fatima Denton and Johan Rockström, prominent voices in their respective fields, lends significant weight to the report’s findings. The report’s focus on AI in military decision-making is particularly relevant given the rapid advancements in the technology and its potential impact on global security. The document's digital version is available at globalchallenges.org/global-catastrophic-risks-2026.

Key Concepts:

  • Earth system tipping points
  • Global governance of nature
  • AI in military decision-making
  • Temporary overshoot in global temperature and Earth system boundaries leading to a safe operating space.
  • Coordinated governance is needed to restore balance across the Earth system through emissions reduction, ecosystem regeneration, and resilience building.
  • Transformations and Earth System Governance are urgently required, extending beyond just decarbonizing the energy system to address interconnected environmental challenges.
  • The Ilulissat Icefjord and Jakobshavn Glacier exhibit a process of ice displacement driven by accumulated snowfall, representing a tipping element within the Earth system.
  • Tipping points differ from traditional climate change due to their potential for abrupt and irreversible planetary reorganization, fundamentally altering governance strategies.
  • The concept of 'tipping elements' highlights the interconnectedness of various Earth system components and the potential for cascading effects when thresholds are crossed.
  • Systems can tolerate impact and recover with minimal negative effects (resilience).
