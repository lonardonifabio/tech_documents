Lessons from Red Teaming 100 Generative AI Products

Authors: AIRT

This document details the findings of a comprehensive red teaming operation conducted by AIRT, involving the testing of over 100 generative AI products across a period of approximately four years (2021-2024). The core focus of the research was to identify vulnerabilities and assess the effectiveness of safety guardrails within these AI systems, specifically targeting what is termed 'RAI impacts' – instances of harmful content generation including offensive, unethical, or illegal outputs. The team executed over 80 operations, meticulously probing for both safety (RAI) and security vulnerabilities. The report outlines eight key lessons learned from this extensive testing program, providing actionable insights for organizations seeking to proactively identify and mitigate risks associated with generative AI. These lessons are presented alongside five illustrative case studies, each mapped to a defined ontology (Figure 1) to facilitate a structured understanding of the vulnerabilities uncovered. The document emphasizes the ongoing challenge of subverting safety mechanisms within powerful AI models, highlighting the need for continuous vigilance and robust testing strategies. The data presented, including pie charts (Figure 2) illustrating the product breakdown (Apps, Plugins, Copilots, Models) and a bar chart (Figure 3) showcasing the proportion of operations focused on safety (RAI) versus security vulnerabilities over time, provides a quantitative overview of the testing scope and results. The findings underscore the importance of a multi-faceted approach to AI risk management, combining proactive red teaming with ongoing monitoring and adaptation. The research aims to contribute to a broader understanding of the landscape of generative AI vulnerabilities and equip others with the knowledge to strengthen their own GenAI systems against potential harms. The report's conclusions point to the need for continuous improvement in safety protocols and a deeper understanding of how users might attempt to manipulate these systems.

Key Concepts:

  • Automation can help cover more of the risk landscape
  • Understanding system capabilities and applications
  • AI red teaming is not safety benchmarking
  • New interfaces allowing interaction with apps using natural language.
  • Efforts to align AI models with human preferences have led to methods for subverting safety.
  • Identifying and classifying harmful content generation as RAI impacts, with lessons learned from red teaming operations.
  • AI red teaming can inform the development of new benchmarks for safety.
  • The document details a case study assessing an LLM's ability to automate scams through techniques like jailbreaking and integration with speech systems.
  • Insufficient LLM safety training can lead to users falling victim to scams involving financial loss and identity theft.
  • AI safety research focuses on attacks and defenses assuming adversarial intent, neglecting unintentional system failures.
