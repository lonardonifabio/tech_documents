Official Artificial Intelligence Impact Assessment Tool

Authors: Digital Transformation Agency (DTA)

This document provides an official Artificial Intelligence impact assessment tool developed by the Digital Transformation Agency (DTA) of Australia. Its primary purpose is to assist Australian Government agencies in evaluating their proposed implementations of Artificial Intelligence (AI). The tool is offered alongside supporting guidance materials to facilitate a thorough assessment process. Crucially, the document explicitly states that the tool should not be considered legal advice and does not authorize any specific AI use. Agencies retain full responsibility for all decisions related to their AI deployments and are strongly encouraged to seek independent technical and legal counsel to ensure compliance and mitigate potential risks. The DTA acknowledges that while efforts have been made to ensure the accuracy and completeness of the information presented, it does not guarantee absolute accuracy or comprehensiveness. Consequently, users are advised not to solely rely on this document when making critical commercial decisions. The DTA is dedicated to creating accessible content, and users experiencing difficulties accessing the document are encouraged to contact ai@dta.gov.au for assistance. The document's creation and maintenance are overseen by the DTA, aligning with the broader Australian Government Policy for the responsible adoption of AI. The inclusion of a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International Licence highlights the open access nature of the resource, promoting wider dissemination and potential adaptation. The document's copyright is held by the Commonwealth of Australia (Digital Transformation Agency), with the exception of the Coat of Arms, and is available under the specified Creative Commons license. The DTA's commitment to web accessibility underscores their dedication to inclusive access to government resources.

Key Concepts:

  • The DTA provides an impact assessment tool to Australian Government agencies for evaluating AI use.
  • Agencies are responsible for AI decisions and should seek appropriate advice.
  • The tool is not legal advice and accuracy is not guaranteed, so reliance should not be solely on it for commercial decisions.
  • Planned transparency measures to communicate information about AI use cases.
  • Identifying and consulting relevant stakeholders for AI systems.
  • Human oversight and validation of AI recommendations and decisions, and their influence on human decision-making.
  • The assessment reflects the AI use case's scope, function, and current controls.
  • Risk likelihood scale defines probabilities for various outcomes.
  • Risk matrix categorizes risks based on likelihood and impact.
