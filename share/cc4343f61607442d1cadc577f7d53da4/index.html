AI Policy Guide and Template

Authors: industry.gov.au/NAIC

This document presents an AI Policy Guide and Template, version 1.0, released in October 2025. It’s designed to assist organizations in developing and maintaining an AI policy, recognizing the importance of responsible AI governance. The guide serves as a foundational resource for all AI-related activities within an organization, outlining a commitment to ethical and consistent AI usage. It provides a structured template with core principles, expectations, and rules for the development, deployment, and use of AI systems. The template aligns with the government’s Guidance for AI Adoption and emphasizes the need for adaptation to reflect an organization’s specific values, industry standards, and legal obligations. Specifically, the document advises users to align statements with core values and mission, define appropriate roles and responsibilities within existing governance structures, and modify terminology to match internal language. The template offers example content to facilitate the initial drafting process, providing a starting point for organizations seeking to establish a robust and compliant AI policy. Ultimately, the goal is to ensure that AI is utilized responsibly and consistently across the organization, addressing potential concerns from stakeholders and auditors. The document highlights the crucial role of an AI policy in establishing a framework for effective AI governance and promoting a trustworthy approach to AI adoption.

Key Concepts:

  • An AI policy is an essential guiding resource for all AI-related activities within an organization.
  • Developing and implementing an AI policy is fundamental to effective AI governance and responsible AI use.
  • The template provides a starting point for creating an AI policy, aligning with government guidance and organizational values.
  • New AI use cases represent potential risks that require documentation and assessment.
  • AI incidents must be reported and managed according to established procedures, including offline system capabilities and manual fallback processes.
  • A defined review cycle is essential for ongoing policy oversight and adaptation.
