Enhancing Explainability in Credit Scoring with Interpretable Machine Learning

Authors: Denis Burakov

This research paper, submitted as part of the XAI-FIN-2023 Submission 6 series, investigates the potential of interpretable machine learning models in credit scoring. The core argument presented is that, under specific design constraints and with responsibly curated data, Machine Learning (ML) models can achieve superior predictive performance – both in terms of discriminatory power and calibration accuracy – compared to traditional linear models. The study utilizes a synthetic dataset generated by combining real-world credit risk data to demonstrate this capability. The paper specifically targets conservative lenders who may be hesitant to adopt more complex algorithms due to concerns about explainability. It suggests that carefully designed ML models can offer a pathway to improved accuracy without sacrificing the crucial ability to understand and justify credit scoring decisions. The research highlights the significant model risk associated with credit scoring, emphasizing that issues like poor model design or the use of non-representative data can lead to inaccurate predictions. The paper underscores the importance of balancing predictive power with interpretability, particularly within a highly regulated industry like financial services. The findings contribute to the ongoing discussion about responsible AI adoption and the need for transparency in credit scoring processes. The research is relevant to Data Science, Machine Learning, and specifically, the application of AI within the Financial Services sector, focusing on the critical area of credit risk assessment.

Key Concepts:

  • Enhancing explainability in credit scoring using interpretable machine learning models.
  • Machine Learning (ML) models can outperform linear alternatives in credit scoring under specific conditions.
  • The design of ML models can be beneficial for conservative lenders transitioning to advanced algorithms while maintaining explainability and accuracy.
  • Pre-binning: A process where data is initially grouped into bins based on certain criteria.
  • XGBoost: An advanced Gradient Tree Boosting software used for large-scale ML tasks.
  • Ensemble Learning: Combining multiple weak learners (decision trees) to create a strong learner.
  • Log Loss: An objective function used in binary classification.
  • Gradient and Hessian Statistics: Used to correct errors in subsequent trees during the boosting process.
  • Tree-based ML models outperform linear alternatives in credit scoring when responsibly designed and constrained.
  • Explainability and accuracy are key considerations in the adoption of ML models for credit risk management.
