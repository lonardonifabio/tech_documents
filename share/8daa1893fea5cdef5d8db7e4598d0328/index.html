Cambridge Taxonomy of Digital Technology Risks

Authors: Kevin Tang, Daniel Ralph, Michelle Tuveson, Trevor Maynard, María Fernanda Lammoglia Cobo

This document presents the Cambridge Taxonomy of Digital Technology Risks, a research report produced by the Cambridge Centre for Risk Studies as part of their Systemic Risks Series. The taxonomy focuses on identifying and categorizing the various risks associated with digital technologies. The research was supported by Risilience Ltd. and benefited from the expertise of a dedicated research team and wider collaborators, including subject matter specialists. Key contributors to the project include Dr. Kevin Tang as the lead researcher, alongside Research Associate Professor Daniel Ralph, Academic Director Dr. Michelle Tuveson, Chairman & Executive Director Dr. Trevor Maynard, and Vice Chair & Director of Systemic Risk Dr. María Fernanda Lammoglia Cobo. The report is a collaborative effort, reflecting the diverse perspectives within the Cambridge Centre for Risk Studies and its connections to the University of Cambridge Judge Business School. The Centre's mission is to investigate and understand systemic risks, particularly those emerging from digital technologies. The document provides a framework for analyzing and mitigating these risks, aiming to contribute to a more resilient and secure digital landscape. It highlights the importance of a comprehensive approach to risk management in the face of rapidly evolving digital technologies. The Centre for Risk Studies operates from Trumpington Street, Cambridge, and maintains an online presence through its website and LinkedIn group, facilitating engagement and knowledge sharing within the risk management community. The views expressed within the report are solely those of the research team and do not represent endorsement by any external entity. The research team's work is central to the Centre's broader goal of advancing understanding and strategies for managing complex systemic risks.

Key Concepts:

  • The Cambridge Centre for Risk Studies has developed a taxonomy of digital technology risks.
  • The research acknowledges contributions from Risilience Ltd. and subject matter specialists.
  • The report represents the views of the Cambridge Centre for Risk Studies research team.
  • The social and economic structure and relationships between social and economic groups are influenced by the creation of new products and services.
  • Periods of industrial revolution are characterized by rapid increases in GDP per capita driven by labor-saving and time-saving technologies.
  • Technological improvements lead to changes in productivity and output, measured by GDP, and are influenced by the combination of changes in labor and capital inputs.
  • The risks of GenAI to business and the economy are uncertain, with some studies suggesting it enhances human capabilities.
  • The primary risk associated with AI is through automation, particularly concerning job displacement.
  • Analysis of AI risks focuses on the potential for automation to displace jobs across various skill levels.
  • Increasing returns to wealth and capital contribute to inequality.
