Agentic AI in Insurance: From Theory to Practice

Authors: David Mitchell-Dawson, Usha Badrinath, Mike Downing, James Fogarty, Hugh Coughlan, Dan Gordon

This document, sourced from TechGlobal, explores the emerging concept of "agentic AI" within the insurance industry, moving beyond theoretical discussions to practical implementation. It highlights key perspectives from industry leaders and technology experts regarding the current state of agentic AI, emphasizing its iterative nature and the importance of early adoption. The excerpt features quotes from individuals at EIP Limited, Mosaic Insurance, WPA Health Insurance, Elio, Fujitsu, and Sonr, illustrating diverse viewpoints on the technology's potential and challenges. A central theme is the need for a strategic approach, advocating for process redesign before applying AI, and recognizing the critical role of data quality and self-improving systems. The discussion underscores the lack of standardization in agentic AI and the significant integration hurdles that early adopters face. The document suggests that governance and adoption frameworks are crucial for successful implementation, framing AI not as a solution to flawed processes, but as a tool to enhance and optimize existing workflows. The core idea is that AI, when applied correctly, can act as a unifying 'glue' across disparate SaaS products within the insurance ecosystem, ultimately driving efficiency and competitive advantage. The excerpt indicates that the field is still in its early stages, with ongoing experimentation and a need for robust frameworks to guide development and deployment.

Key Concepts:

  • Agentic AI is in its early stages and experts are still learning.
  • Data quality is a key focus for agentic AI systems, contributing to self-improvement.
  • Effective AI implementation in insurance requires rethinking existing processes before applying AI.
  • Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) allows models to access and read proprietary data sources for higher accuracy.
  • Prompt engineering is a simple approach using carefully crafted instructions to base models.
  • Agentic AI adoption faces challenges related to data fragmentation, inconsistent quality, and siloed systems.
  • Agentic AI is an achievable evolution for the insurance industry requiring a pragmatic and collaborative approach.
  • Successful AI adoption necessitates a methodical process of organizational learning, strategic alignment, and disciplined execution.
  • Starting with small, focused use cases with measurable outcomes is crucial for early wins and controlled learning.
