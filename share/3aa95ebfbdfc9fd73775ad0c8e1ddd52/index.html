Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content

This document excerpt outlines the first draft of a Code of Practice concerning the transparency of AI-generated content, scheduled for implementation in December 2025. The primary goal of this Code is to enhance the operation of the internal market and encourage the adoption of human-centric and trustworthy artificial intelligence (AI) systems. Simultaneously, it prioritizes the safeguarding of health, safety, and fundamental rights. The document is structured around key sections, including ‘How To Read This Document,’ an ‘Explainer’ section offering a summary and critical analysis of the requirements, a ‘Legal’ section referencing relevant legal provisions (specifically Article 50(2) and (5) of the AI Act), and ‘Section 1’ which details the ‘Rules for Marking and Detection of AI-Generated and Manipulated Content.’ It’s designed for providers of AI systems, focusing on establishing clear guidelines for identifying and labeling content created using AI, as well as methods for detecting manipulation. The document’s structure suggests a comprehensive approach to managing the risks and ensuring responsible development and deployment of AI technologies. The inclusion of appendices and a detailed table of contents indicates a substantial and well-organized document intended for practical application and reference. The document's focus on the AI Act highlights the regulatory landscape surrounding AI development and usage, emphasizing the need for transparency and accountability. The overarching theme is to balance innovation with ethical considerations and consumer protection within the AI ecosystem. Further sections (Section 2 and the Appendix) are referenced, suggesting a more detailed exploration of the topic.

Key Concepts:

  • This Code aims to improve the internal market and promote trustworthy AI.
  • The Code focuses on marking and detecting AI-generated and manipulated content.
  • The Code ensures a high level of protection of health, safety, and fundamental rights.
  • Metadata embedding provides information about AI operation types (prompting, editing, generation).
  • Multi-layered machine-readable marking ensures transparency and traceability of AI outputs.
  • Imperceptible watermarks interwoven within content provide robust identification of AI-generated or manipulated content.
  • Detectors for already marked AI-generated content are provided by Signatories to facilitate downstream compliance.
  • Signatories will offer free interfaces or APIs for users and third parties to verify content generation by AI systems with confidence scores.
  • Maintaining detectors throughout the system’s lifecycle and ensuring legacy content remains detectable are encouraged to support traceability.
