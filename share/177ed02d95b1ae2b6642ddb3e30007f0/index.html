Can AI grow green? Evidence of an inverted -U curve between AI, energy use and emissions

Authors: AngelMelguizo, Raúl Katz, Juan Jung

This working paper, published in June 2025 by the Technology and Industrialisation for Development Centre (TIDE), investigates the relationship between Artificial Intelligence (AI), energy consumption, and greenhouse gas emissions. The research explores an inverted-U curve, suggesting that as AI adoption increases, energy use and emissions may initially rise but eventually decline due to efficiency gains and technological advancements. The TIDE Centre, established in 2008 by Professor Xiaolan Fu, is a multidisciplinary research hub within the University of Oxford’s Department of International Development. The centre’s origins trace back to pioneering work by influential development economists like Frances Stewart, Sanjaya Lall, Barbara Harriss-White, Adrian Wood, and Valpy FitzGerald, who recognized technology and industrialization as crucial drivers of sustainable development, particularly in addressing concerns around food security, poverty reduction, and aid. The rebranding to the Technology and Industrialisation forDevelopment Centre (TIDE) in 2024 reflects the centre's ongoing commitment to understanding the complex interplay between technology, industrialization, trade, investment, and inclusive prosperity, especially within the context of the global transition to a low-carbon economy and the intensifying competition in global industrialization. The research highlights the importance of strategically deploying AI to maximize its potential for reducing environmental impact, acknowledging the potential for initial increases in energy use followed by long-term reductions. The centre’s work is particularly relevant given the growing disparities in technological capabilities and the need for sustainable development strategies. The paper likely presents empirical evidence supporting this inverted-U curve hypothesis, contributing to the broader discussion on the role of AI in achieving environmental sustainability.
