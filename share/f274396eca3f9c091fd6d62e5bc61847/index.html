AI Governance around the world: European Union

Authors: Arcangelo Leone de Castris

This document, published in 2025 by Arcangelo Leone de Castris, provides a country profile focusing on AI governance within the European Union. It’s produced by The Alan Turing Institute, the UK’s national institute for data science and artificial intelligence. The report acknowledges contributions from Matthieu Binder and Franziska Busse from the Zentrum für Vertrauenswürdige KI. The core argument centers on the evolving international AI governance landscape, driven by nations recognizing AI's strategic importance for economic prosperity and national security. This recognition has spurred increased investment in domestic AI infrastructure, leading to a more competitive global environment. However, the document stresses the continued necessity of international trade and cooperation to maximize the economic advantages of AI while simultaneously mitigating potential risks. Specifically, it highlights the translation of high-level AI principles into tangible policies and the development of distinct regulatory frameworks. The report implicitly addresses the challenges of balancing innovation with responsible AI development, a key concern in the rapidly changing field of artificial intelligence. The document’s publication date, 1 August 2025, suggests a forward-looking perspective on the ongoing debates and developments surrounding AI governance globally, particularly within the EU’s unique approach to regulation and technological advancement. The inclusion of a DOI (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16779366) indicates a formal publication and accessibility for scholarly research. The Alan Turing Institute's involvement further positions this as a credible and authoritative source of information on this topic.
