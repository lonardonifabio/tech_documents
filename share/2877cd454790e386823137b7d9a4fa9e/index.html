Serious Incident Reporting: Ensuring Public Safety

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Félicien Vallet, Shraeyas Jayasimha R, Juhi Koré, Jeremy Prasetyo EU AI Act

This document, titled "Serious Incident Reporting: Ensuring Public Safety," analyzes emerging trends in AI incident reporting, primarily focusing on data collected between May – September 2025. The core finding is that accountability and transparency are the most frequently reported areas within AI incidents, representing 336 reports in September 2025. Transparency, safety, and robustness also receive significant attention, though recent declines may indicate improvements in system resilience or changes in reporting criteria. Notably, concerns regarding fairness, human wellbeing, and sustainability consistently lag behind, suggesting potential gaps in risk assessment. The report emphasizes the need for more comprehensive AI risk management frameworks that go beyond traditional governance and safety considerations to encompass social, ethical, and environmental impacts. The data presented offers valuable insights for proactive reporting, particularly in identifying areas where serious incidents could arise. The analysis is directly related to the evolving landscape of the EU AI Act and aims to improve public safety by highlighting the importance of a holistic approach to AI risk management, incorporating a broader range of ethical and sustainability considerations. The document suggests that a balanced and proactive reporting framework is crucial to address the full spectrum of potential risks associated with AI systems, moving beyond simply focusing on compliance with regulations like Article 73 of the EU AI Act.

Key Concepts:

  • Evolving priorities in AI incident monitoring
  • Underreported ethical and sustainability risks
  • Need for clearer definitions and stronger reporting mechanisms for underreported risks
  • Importance of proactive reporting frameworks encompassing social, ethical, and environmental dimensions of AI impact
  • Responsibility for reporting serious incidents lies with the AI system provider.
  • Deployer obligations depend on the incident and contractual arrangements.
  • Reporting deadlines exist based on the nature of the incident (15 days, 2 days, 10 days).
  • Transparency and investigation are crucial for building trust and addressing regulatory concerns.
  • Providers must take immediate and thorough corrective actions after a serious incident.
  • Public Interest Harm: The potential for AI to negatively impact society and erode trust.
