AI Model Evaluations and Reporting

This document explores the evolving landscape of AI model evaluations and the associated reporting practices surrounding them. The research investigates a substantial dataset of 186 AI models, complemented by post-release reporting data sourced from 183 sources including websites and GitHub repositories. A key focus is on comparing first-party and third-party reporting, dissecting the differences across various dimensions such as social impact categories, model openness (open vs. closed), sector of origin (academia, government, industry, non-profit), geographic region, and publication/release date. The analysis highlights the increasing importance of AI evaluations – encompassing systematic assessments of model behavior in context – and benchmarks, which provide standardized datasets and metrics for comparable evaluations. However, the document critically examines the current state of evaluation practice, noting a frequent lack of scientific rigor, characterized by a deficiency in systematic, transparent, and reproducible methodologies. This often results in insufficient information for further use and disparate evaluation methods across leaderboards. The research acknowledges the common use of the term "foundation model," defined as pre-trained generative AI models lacking a specific application context. The document underscores the need for more robust and standardized evaluation practices within the AI model lifecycle, impacting development, deployment, policy decisions, and practitioner choices. The analysis implicitly calls for greater transparency and reproducibility in evaluation methodologies to ensure reliable and comparable assessments of AI models.

Key Concepts:

  • Foundation models are increasingly central to high-stakes AI systems, and governance frameworks now depend on evaluations to assess their risks and capabilities.
  • Social impact assessments covering bias, fairness, privacy, environmental costs, and labor practices remain uneven across the AI ecosystem.
  • A comprehensive analysis of both first-party and third-party social impact evaluation reporting across a wide range of model developers was conducted.
  • Systematic assessment of model behavior in context is defined as evaluation.
  • Benchmarks are standardized datasets, tasks, and metrics for comparable model evaluations.
  • Evaluation practice often lacks scientific rigor, requiring systematic, transparent, and reproducible methodology.
  • Developers' motivations for conducting and reporting social impact evaluations.
  • Barriers preventing developers from conducting social impact evaluations.
  • Organizational and structural factors shaping evaluation and reporting practices.
