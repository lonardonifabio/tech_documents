LLM Classification of Legal and Governance Documents - AI Risk Taxonomy

Authors: MIT (airisk), Unknown

This document details a research project conducted by MIT's airisk team investigating the application of Large Language Models (LLMs) for classifying legal and governance documents related to AI risks. The core of the study involved evaluating the performance of several LLMs – Claude Sonnet 4.5, Claude Opus 4.1, and GPT-5 – alongside human reviewers, specifically focusing on documents sourced from the ETO AGORA archive. The team initially tested a comparative approach across six documents, assessing the agreement between LLM classifications and human consensus. Subsequently, they scaled up their analysis significantly, utilizing Claude Sonnet 4.5 to classify over 950 governance documents, categorizing them according to a diverse set of taxonomies encompassing AI risks, mitigation strategies, and associated governance frameworks. The research centered on identifying prevalent and underrepresented risk domains within the analyzed documents. Key findings revealed that Claude Sonnet 4.5, Claude Opus 4.1, and GPT-5 demonstrated comparable or superior agreement with human consensus compared to independent human classifications for the initial six documents. Specifically, the most frequently addressed risk subdomains were identified as Governance failure, AI system security vulnerabilities & attacks, and Lack of transparency or interpretability. Conversely, areas like AI Welfare and Rights, Multi-agent risks, and Economic and cultural devaluation of human effort received comparatively less attention. The study also highlighted sector-specific variations in coverage, with Public Administration (excluding National Security), Scientific R&D, and National Security exhibiting the highest levels of documentation, while sectors such as Accommodation, Food, and Other Services, Arts, Entertainment, and Recreation, and Real Estate and Rental and Leasing were significantly underrepresented. The research team is currently seeking feedback and expressions of interest in further collaboration. The project’s ultimate goal is to establish a robust and scalable method for automatically classifying and understanding the complex landscape of AI governance risks, potentially informing policy development and risk mitigation strategies. The use of LLMs offers a promising avenue for efficiently processing and analyzing the growing volume of information related to AI governance.

Key Concepts:

  • Using LLMs to categorize legal and governance documents
  • Comparison of LLM performance versus human reviewers in classifying documents
  • Identification of key AI risk subdomains (Governance failure, AI system security vulnerabilities & attacks, Lack of transparency or interpretability)
  • The AGORA dataset provides a foundation for mapping AI governance, but has limitations regarding scope and generalizability.
  • The dataset's heavy U.S. weighting leads to an overrepresentation of U.S. governance trends, limiting its global applicability.
  • Language constraints due to the dataset's reliance on English prevent comprehensive testing of LLM interpretation of governance texts in other languages, highlighting the need for multilingual support.
  • A staged self-governance regime for frontier AI development.
  • Establishing governance measures for frontier AI development through a CalCompute Consortium.
  • A policy governing municipal use of AI, incorporating principles of effectiveness, transparency, equity, and accountability.
