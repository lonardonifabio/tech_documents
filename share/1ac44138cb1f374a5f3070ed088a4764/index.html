AI and the Law: Discussion Paper

Authors: National Archives, Law Commission

This discussion paper, published by the National Archives and the Law Commission in 2025, explores the complex legal implications arising from the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI). The document delves into key areas of concern, including the legal challenges posed by AI’s increasing autonomy, adaptability, and potential for error. It examines how AI systems, such as AlphaFold 3, which has achieved groundbreaking accuracy in protein structure prediction – significantly surpassing traditional methods – are impacting scientific research and potentially influencing legal frameworks. The paper addresses critical questions surrounding liability when AI systems cause harm, particularly concerning the ‘mental element’ required for establishing culpability. It investigates the difficulties in determining causation when AI systems make decisions, and the challenges of assigning responsibility in situations where AI systems operate with a degree of autonomy. The discussion paper also tackles the issue of opacity – the ‘black box’ nature of many AI algorithms – and the difficulties in understanding how AI systems arrive at their conclusions. Furthermore, it considers the implications of AI’s increasing reliance on data and the potential for bias within training datasets. A central theme is the question of whether AI systems should be granted separate legal personality, a concept that raises fundamental questions about accountability and legal rights. The document highlights the need for robust oversight mechanisms to ensure that AI systems are used responsibly and ethically, and to mitigate the risks associated with their deployment. The paper’s analysis is informed by recent advancements in AI, including the Nobel Prize-winning AlphaFold system, and the ongoing development of sophisticated AI technologies. It emphasizes the urgency of developing legal frameworks that can adapt to the rapidly evolving landscape of AI, balancing innovation with the protection of individual rights and societal well-being. The discussion paper serves as a foundational resource for understanding the legal challenges and opportunities presented by AI, and for informing the development of appropriate regulatory responses.
