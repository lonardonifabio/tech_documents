Losing Freedom

Authors: Du Feichen

This document, "Losing Freedom," explores the intersection of artificial intelligence (AI) harms and human rights, offering a high-level examination of the complex relationship between the two. It begins with an introduction to the rapid growth of AI adoption, citing projections from the International Monetary Fund and Goldman Sachs regarding AI's impact on employment and global GDP. The core argument highlights the need to balance AI's potential economic benefits with a critical assessment of its potential harms. The document draws upon the Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, emphasizing a relative lack of focused research on mapping AI harms against fundamental human rights. Specifically, it addresses the challenge of identifying and categorizing the diverse ways in which AI systems can infringe upon human rights. The paper proposes a practical, adaptable table-based approach for mapping these interactions, intended for use by both public and private entities. This approach aims to facilitate a systematic evaluation of AI systems to determine their potential impact on human rights, prompting consideration of whether an AI system could pose a risk to fundamental rights. The document implicitly acknowledges the growing body of academic literature and international discourse surrounding AI harms, while advocating for a more structured and actionable methodology for assessing and mitigating these risks. The focus is on proactively identifying and addressing potential harms before widespread AI deployment, ensuring alignment with democratic values and the rule of law.

Key Concepts:

  • The use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) is rapidly increasing and has the potential to significantly impact the global economy.
  • AI presents potential harms that must be considered alongside its benefits.
  • There is a need to map the relationship between AI harms and fundamental human rights.
  • The rapid uptake of AI systems by States and businesses is creating potential for adverse impacts on human rights.
  • AI systems themselves may not cause harm if they do not operate autonomously or have real-world implications.
  • Highly capable general purpose AI models pose a significant risk of harm, either deliberate or unintentional.
  • Everyone has the right to equality and freedom from discrimination on protected grounds.
  • Discrimination can include any distinction, exclusion, restriction, or preference, which can impair people’s enjoyment of rights and freedoms.
  • AI systems can lead to harm when their output results in differential treatment based on protected characteristics.
  • Threats to life, liberty, or security due to AI output
