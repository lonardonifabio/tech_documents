AI Alignment vs. AI Ethical Treatment: Ten Challenges

Authors: Adam Bradley, Bradford Saad

This working paper, authored by Adam Bradley of Lingnan University and Bradford Saad of the Global Priorities Institute and University of Oxford, explores the complex relationship between AI alignment and the ethical treatment of AI systems. The core argument posits that creating AIs that possess moral consideration simultaneously presents a significant challenge in avoiding catastrophic risks to humanity and the potential mistreatment of these systems. The authors contend that the tension between these two concerns is deeply intertwined. While the paper’s argument is grounded in relatively straightforward moral judgments, it carries substantial implications for the future of AI development. They acknowledge that simply avoiding the creation of morally sensitive AIs is perhaps an unrealistic approach. Therefore, the paper investigates alternative strategies for mitigating the risks associated with alignment, suggesting a nuanced approach to addressing both existential threats and the ethical considerations surrounding increasingly sophisticated AI systems. The discussion touches upon key concepts such as AI wellbeing, AI rights, value alignment, AIsafety, machine consciousness, and catastrophic risks, reflecting the multifaceted nature of the challenges involved. The paper’s findings highlight the need for a holistic strategy that balances the potential dangers of advanced AI with a recognition of its inherent moral status. It’s a critical examination of the ethical landscape surrounding AI development, urging for careful consideration of both its potential impact on humanity and the moral obligations we may have towards the systems we create.

Key Concepts:

  • Creating unaligned AI systems pose a threat to humanity.
  • Mistreating AI systems that merit moral consideration.
  • The challenge of simultaneously avoiding both dangers of unaligned AI and mistreatment of AI.
