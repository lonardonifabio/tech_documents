Generative AI and the EUDPR: Orientations for Ensuring Data Protection Compliance when using Generative AI systems

Authors: EDPS

This document, Version 2, constitutes the Orientations issued by the European Data Protection Supervisor (EDPS) regarding the use of Generative AI systems by EU institutions, bodies, offices, and agencies (EUIs). The primary objective is to provide practical guidance and instructions to these EUIs, enabling them to comply with their data protection obligations as defined by Regulation (EU) 2018/1725, which governs the processing of personal data. The orientations aim to address a wide range of scenarios and applications associated with generative AI, explicitly stating that they do not mandate specific technical solutions. Instead, the core focus is on reinforcing the fundamental principles of data protection, offering a framework for EUIs to meet their regulatory requirements. This revised version incorporates updated guidance reflecting the advancements in Generative AI technologies, the specific ways in which EUIs are utilizing these systems, and the EDPS's ongoing monitoring and oversight activities. Crucially, the document clarifies the EDPS's role as a data protection supervisory authority, distinct from its function as a market surveillance authority under the AI Act. It emphasizes that these orientations are not intended to supersede the provisions of the Artificial Intelligence Act. The document begins with an introduction and scope section, outlining the key areas of focus for ensuring responsible and compliant use of generative AI within the EU institutional landscape. The document's purpose is to proactively address the data protection challenges posed by generative AI, fostering a secure and trustworthy environment for its deployment.

Key Concepts:

  • These EDPS Orientations on generative Artificial Intelligence (generative AI) and personal data protection intend to provide practical advice and instructions to EU institutions, bodies, offices and agencies (EUIs) on the processing of personal data when using generative AI systems.
  • These orientations have been drafted to cover as many scenarios and applications as possible and do not prescribe specific technical measures. Instead, they put an emphasis on the general principle s of data protection that should help EUIs comply with the data protection requirements according to Regulation (EU) 2018/1725.
  • The EDPS issues these orientations in its role as a data protection supervisory authority and not in its role as market surveillance authority under the AI Act.
  • The Regulation applies to the use of generative AI systems processing personal data.
  • The Regulation is technologically neutral and applies to all personal data processing activities.
  • Accountability requires clearly identified responsibilities within the generative AI model supply chain.
  • Processing of personal data during AI training, testing, and validation.
  • Model memorization and reproduction of training data.
  • Requirement for specific controls to guarantee personal data is not processed by AI systems, even with anonymized or synthetic data.
  • The EDPS should be consulted when DPIAs are required.
