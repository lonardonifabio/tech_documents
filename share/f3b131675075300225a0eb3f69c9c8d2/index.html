Belgium's Approach to AI Governance and the AI Act

Authors: Belgium Government, EU Commission (implied)

This document outlines Belgium's strategy for navigating the evolving landscape of Artificial Intelligence, primarily in relation to the EU's AI Act. Belgium recognizes the significant potential of AI as a driver for digital transformation and public benefit, particularly within its large enterprises and public sector. The country's approach to AI governance will be closely aligned with EU law, specifically the AI Act, emphasizing a risk-based regulatory framework. Key stakeholders, including sector-specific regulators and the Belgian Data Protection Authority (DPA), will play crucial roles in overseeing AI development and deployment, with a strong focus on personal data and automated decision-making. Belgium currently lacks a formal legal definition of AI within its national legislation, intending to adopt the definition provided in Article 3(1) of the EU AI Act as the governing standard. The document highlights the integration of ethical principles – fairness, accountability, and proportionality – into strategic initiatives like the AI4Belgium initiative and the Convergence Plan, promoting a cautious and inclusive approach to AI development. Existing Belgian legal frameworks, such as the GDPR (as implemented nationally), product liability laws, and consumer protection regulations, will continue to shape the deployment of AI technologies. Furthermore, Belgium anticipates ongoing evolution of its AI governance model in conjunction with EU developments, prioritizing safe, transparent, and human-centric AI systems. The document underscores a commitment to balancing innovation with robust accountability measures, reflecting a proactive stance towards responsible AI adoption.
