Guidelines on Scope of Obligations for GPAI Models

Authors: European Commission

This document, released by the European Commission, outlines guidelines concerning the scope of obligations associated with General Purpose AI (GPAI) models. Specifically, it addresses the classification and notification requirements for these models. The core focus is on defining what constitutes a general-purpose AI model and subsequently assessing the potential systemic risk associated with such models. The guidelines establish criteria for determining when a model is considered a general-purpose AI model, emphasizing the importance of understanding the entire lifecycle of these models – from development to deployment. A key element is the classification process, which aims to identify models posing systemic risk. This classification triggers specific notification requirements, demanding transparency and accountability from developers and deployers. The document meticulously details the classification criteria, including factors related to the model's capabilities, potential impact, and the context of its use. It’s a crucial step in the European Union’s efforts to regulate AI, promoting responsible innovation and mitigating potential harms. The guidelines are designed to foster trust and confidence in AI systems while ensuring alignment with broader societal values. The document’s structure is designed to provide a clear framework for understanding and complying with the evolving regulatory landscape for GPAI models. It’s a foundational piece for anyone involved in the development, deployment, or use of these powerful technologies within the EU. The document’s emphasis on lifecycle management underscores the need for continuous monitoring and adaptation as AI models evolve. The notification process is central to the regulatory approach, requiring proactive disclosure of information to facilitate oversight and risk assessment. This framework is intended to balance innovation with safeguards, ultimately contributing to a safer and more trustworthy AI ecosystem. The document’s release in August 2025 indicates a forward-looking approach, anticipating future developments in the field of AI.
