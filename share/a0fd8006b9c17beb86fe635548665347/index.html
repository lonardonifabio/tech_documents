Doing AI Differently

This white paper, titled "Doing AI Differently," advocates for a fundamental rethinking of Artificial Intelligence (AI) development, emphasizing the crucial role of the humanities. The document addresses several key challenges within the current AI landscape, primarily focusing on the need for a more nuanced and human-centered approach. It identifies the ‘homogenisation problem’ – the tendency for AI development to prioritize efficiency and standardized solutions, often neglecting qualitative aspects and diverse human needs. The core argument centers around a ‘qualitative turn,’ suggesting that AI should move beyond purely quantitative analysis and incorporate insights from the humanities to better understand and address complex human problems. The paper explores the transformation of human cognition as a central element in this shift, arguing that AI systems should be designed with a deeper understanding of how humans think, learn, and interact. It proposes a transdisciplinary endeavor, bringing together experts from various fields – including computer science, philosophy, sociology, and psychology – to tackle these challenges. The document outlines specific objectives related to developing interpretative technologies and exploring alternative AI architectures that are more aligned with human values and capabilities. Ultimately, "Doing AI Differently" calls for a more holistic and ethically grounded approach to AI development, recognizing the profound impact of AI on society and the importance of incorporating human perspectives throughout the design and implementation process. The paper’s intent is to stimulate a broader conversation about the future of AI and to promote a more responsible and beneficial trajectory for this rapidly evolving technology. It’s a call to action for researchers, developers, and policymakers to consider the broader implications of AI and to prioritize human-centric design principles.

Key Concepts:

  • Rethinking the foundations of AI via the humanities
  • The qualitative turn in AI
  • The homogenisation problem in AI
  • Artificial Intelligence is rapidly becoming global infrastructure shaping decisions in various sectors.
  • Current AI systems are limited by narrow operational metrics failing to capture human experience.
  • Interpretive depth is essential for building AI systems capable of engaging with cultural complexity.
  • AI systems lack frameworks for interpreting cultural content they generate and encounter.
  • Integrating humanities into AI design
  • Humanities as foundational design input
  • Protecting cultural meaning-making from computational treatment
