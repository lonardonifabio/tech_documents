EU AI Act Advancing Data Governance

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli

This report examines how Article 10 of the EU AI Act, specifically concerning data and data governance, directly supports the objectives of the Data Governance Act. The core focus is on bolstering trust in data sharing, actively strengthening the mechanisms designed to increase data availability across various sectors, and crucially, addressing the significant technical barriers that have historically hindered the effective reuse of data within the context of AI systems. The document highlights the importance of a robust regulatory framework – as represented by the EU AI Act – in facilitating a more open and trustworthy data ecosystem. It emphasizes that the EU AI Act's provisions are not merely theoretical; they are intended to create a tangible shift in how data is managed and utilized, ultimately driving innovation and responsible AI development. The report underscores the need for proactive measures to overcome technical obstacles, promoting greater data accessibility and encouraging the development of AI systems that are built on a foundation of trust. Furthermore, the document implicitly advocates for a collaborative approach, suggesting that successful implementation requires engagement with industry groups like AI Commons. The report’s authors, Sean Musch and Michael Borrelli from AI & Partners, clearly articulate their organization’s mission: to defend and extend the digital rights of users at risk globally, leveraging a multi-faceted strategy encompassing technical support, policy engagement, advocacy, and participation in industry groups. The report’s underlying message is that responsible AI development – one that prioritizes trust and avoids potential harm – is not just a desirable outcome, but a necessary condition for long-term success in the artificial intelligence age. The authors’ belief that AI should unlock potential rather than cause harm is central to their approach, and they aim to assist organizations in building AI systems that can be both trusted and sustainable.
