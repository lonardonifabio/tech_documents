Mapping AI Risk Mitigations: Evidence Scan and Preliminary AI Risk Mitigation Taxonomy

Authors: Alexander K. Saeri, Sophia Lloyd George, Jess Graham, Clelia D. Lacarriere, Peter Slattery, Michael Noete l, Neil Thompson

This research paper addresses the fragmented and inconsistent nature of existing AI risk mitigation frameworks, highlighting the need for a standardized approach. The document introduces a preliminary AI Risk Mitigation Taxonomy, developed through a rapid evidence scan of 13 AI risk mitigation frameworks published between 2023 and 2025. This scan resulted in a living database of 831 distinct AI risk mitigations, which were then iteratively clustered and coded to create the taxonomy. The taxonomy organizes these mitigations into four key categories: (1) Governance & Oversight, which focuses on establishing human oversight mechanisms and decision protocols through formal organizational structures and policy frameworks; (2) Technical & Security, concentrating on technical, physical, and engineering safeguards designed to secure AI systems and constrain model behaviors; (3) Operational Process, dealing with processes and management frameworks governing the deployment, usage, monitoring, incident handling, and validation of AI systems; and (4) Tra – the last category is incomplete. The research emphasizes the importance of coordinated efforts by organizations and governments to effectively mitigate AI risks, aiming to provide a common frame of reference for risk mitigation strategies. The paper's methodology – a comprehensive evidence scan and iterative coding process – demonstrates a systematic approach to developing a practical taxonomy for navigating the complex landscape of AI risk management. The goal is to improve clarity and facilitate collaboration in the field of AI risk mitigation, ultimately contributing to the responsible development and deployment of artificial intelligence technologies. The document's findings underscore the need for a structured approach to managing the potential risks associated with AI, promoting safer and more reliable AI systems.

Key Concepts:

  • The need for coordinated risk mitigation across organizations and governments regarding AI development and deployment.
  • A fragmented landscape of AI risk mitigation frameworks with inconsistent terminology and gaps in coverage.
  • A preliminary AI Risk Mitigation Taxonomy developed through a rapid evidence scan and iterative clustering of 831 AI risk mitigations categorized into Governance & Oversight, Technical & Security, Operational Process, and Tra
  • The document utilizes the MIT AI Risk Taxonomy to categorize AI risk mitigations.
  • A socio-technical approach is employed, distinguishing between AI models and human behavior as targets of mitigation.
  • The taxonomy evolved through iterative classification and feedback, ultimately resulting in a four-category and 23-subcategory structure.
  • The high prevalence of ‘I don’t know’ responses regarding enterprise risk management suggests a lack of understanding of the concept.
  • ‘AI risk management’ is an emerging concept with undefined boundaries and a contested definition.
  • Risk management encompasses identifying, analyzing, enacting measures, governing, and monitoring risks, as demonstrated by various mitigation subcategories.
  • Towards best practices in AGI safety and governance, focusing on expert opinion.
