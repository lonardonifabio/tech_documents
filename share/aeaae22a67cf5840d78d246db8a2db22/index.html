A Human-Centred Approach to Scaling AI Tools: The Assist Case Study

Authors: Government Communications

This document details the UK Government Communications' experience with Assist, the first general-purpose AI tool approved for government-wide use. Assist, developed in-house by the GCS Applied Data and Insight Team, is a generative AI tool designed to transform government communication workflows. Launched in November 2023, the Assist pilot has already yielded significant benefits, including increased productivity for communicators (saving thousands of hours) and improved integration of communications best practices through embedding government frameworks. The tool has been utilized across over 200 government organizations, facilitating the rapid delivery of high-quality, consistent public sector communications. Recognizing the valuable lessons learned during this scaling process, Government Communications is sharing its insights and resources, including the Mitigating Hidden AI Risks Toolkit, to assist other organizations facing similar challenges in implementing generative AI tools. The core of the document highlights a human-centered approach, emphasizing the importance of safe and successful scaling of GenAI tools. The document serves as a practical guide and knowledge base for organizations looking to adopt and scale GenAI solutions, focusing on a framework built on evidence and best practices. The ultimate goal is to promote responsible and effective implementation of GenAI across various sectors, ensuring alignment with government policies and communication standards.

Key Concepts:

  • Human-centred approach to AI scaling
  • Evidence-based framework for AI scaling
  • Secure and bespoke AI tool development
  • Integration of communications best practice
  • Successful and safe GenAI scaling
  • Unknown unknowns: Risks stemming from unforeseen and unpredictable factors.
  • Human fallibility: Recognizing that humans, like machines, are susceptible to errors and limitations.
  • Interdisciplinary approach: The need for collaboration across different fields to address AI safety effectively.
  • Build-up of small risks: The potential for significant risks to arise from accumulating minor oversights.
  • Understanding uptake and high-quality use of GenAI tools is vital for success and safety.
