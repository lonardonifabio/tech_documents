Guide on AI and Human Rights - Methodology

Authors: ECNL, DIHR

This document outlines the methodology behind a guide concerning the intersection of Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights. The guide was developed with funding from the European Artificial Intelligence & Society Fund and is released under a Creative Commons license (CC-BY SA 4.0). The core of the guide’s development stems from materials and experiences provided by ECNL and DIHR, significantly informed by input from human rights experts and civil society organizations. Three workshops were conducted in Berlin, Copenhagen, and Brussels to gather this input, focusing on the impact of AI on fundamental rights. The methodology heavily relies on a review of academic literature, including examples cited within the document, such as the Fundamental Rights and Algorithms Impact Assessment used by the Government of Netherlands. Specifically, the Excel FRIA Template, particularly its context analysis section, was developed through a careful examination of similar questionnaires employed in AI impact assessment methodologies. The document acknowledges the contributions of several key individuals and organizations, including Algorithm Watch and Michele Loi, who provided substantial input to the questionnaire template. Notable contributors include Gianclaudio Malgieri (eLaw, Leiden University), Isabelle Schipper (Netherlands Institute for Human Rights), Iris Muis and Julia Straatman (Utrecht Data School), Patricia Shaw (Beyond Reach Consulting Limited), and numerous civil society colleagues. The document’s foundation is built upon a rigorous process of research, consultation, and template development, aiming to provide a practical tool for assessing the potential human rights implications of AI systems. It represents a collaborative effort to address a critical area of concern within the rapidly evolving field of artificial intelligence.

Key Concepts:

  • This Guide is based on ECNL and DIHR materials and experiences.
  • The document acknowledges contributions from Algorithm Watch, human rights experts, and civil society organizations.
  • The methodology utilizes workshops, academic literature, and existing questionnaires for AI impact assessment.
  • Meaningful participation of rights-holders and their proxies in AI impact assessments and mitigation design.
  • Consideration of barriers to participation (geographic, digital literacy, resources) when consulting with rights-holders.
  • Accountability for FRIA implementation, monitoring, and documentation within an organization.
  • Systematic underestimates of distress situations due to lack of training data.
  • Over-flagging of routine maritime activities leading to misdirected resources.
  • Use of 'irregular migration risk' scores to justify push-back policies without legal process.
  • The complaint mechanism should be proactively communicated to potentially affected individuals.
