EU AI Act Implementation - 2025

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Lisa McClory

This document outlines the key milestones and developments surrounding the implementation of the EU AI Act in 2025. Beginning in February 2025, the European Union initiated a series of rapid actions to address the potential risks associated with Artificial Intelligence. These actions included the prohibition of risky AI practices, the establishment of basic AI literacy requirements across the EU, and the implementation of rules specifically targeting general-purpose AI (GPAI) models and notified bodies. A crucial element was the launch of the ‘Apply AI’ support strategy in October 2025, alongside the creation of an AI Act Service Desk to provide assistance to AI providers. Simultaneously, the Commission published the ‘Digital Omnibus’ on AI, initiating a process aimed at simplifying the EU AI Act. The development and finalization of the initial codes of practice, designed to guide compliance for AI providers, was also a key focus. Looking ahead to 2025, the EU AI Act readiness demonstrated a significant disparity across the EU27. While a select group of nations – Denmark, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Malta, and Spain – achieved ‘Green’ status by enacting national frameworks and establishing the necessary authorities for enforcement, the majority of Member States remained in the ‘Amber’ category, with ongoing efforts to draft legislation, establish partial authority listings, or develop the required structures. The document highlights the coordinated effort by the European Commission to establish a regulatory framework for AI within the EU, emphasizing compliance, support, and the gradual adoption of the AI Act across member states. The timeline provided (2 February, 2 August, 19 November, 2 May) indicates the phased rollout of these initiatives, reflecting the complex nature of implementing such a transformative legislation.

Key Concepts:

  • The EU is implementing a rapid overhaul of AI practices, including bans, codes of conduct, and compliance rules.
  • EU AI Act readiness varies significantly across member states, with a small group achieving 'Green' status and the majority progressing through 'Amber' stages.
  • The Commission is publishing the Digital Omnibus to simplify the EU AI Act and finalizing codes of practice to guide AI provider compliance.
  • The AI Act's effectiveness hinges on consistent and robust implementation to prevent fragmentation.
  • A robust, enforceable European standard for trustworthy AI is crucial in a power-shaped digital economy.
  • National authorities require adequate resources (funding, staffing, expertise) to effectively implement and enforce the AI Act.
  • Designation of BNetzA as primary market surveillance authority
  • BaFin as AI authority for high-risk financial services
  • Establishment of interagency coordination center (KoKIVO) and KI Service Desk
  • Greece's implementation of national authorities for protecting fundamental rights under AI
