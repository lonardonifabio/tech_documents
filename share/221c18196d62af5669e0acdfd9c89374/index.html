A Framework for AI Benefit-Sharing

Authors: Unknown

This paper addresses the critical issue of equitable global access to the benefits of Artificial Intelligence (AI), highlighting the risk that current development trajectories and the existing AI governance ecosystem are failing to include billions, particularly those in Global Majority countries. The rapid advancement of AI, occurring in months rather than decades, presents unprecedented opportunities for innovation and economic growth, yet this progress is threatened by a lack of systematic mechanisms for ensuring fair global AI diffusion and managing associated risks. To rectify this gap, the paper proposes a comprehensive framework for AI benefit-sharing, centered around the fair distribution and access to AI's opportunities and gains under conditions of safety. This framework is built upon three core pillars: firstly, the redistribution of economic gains generated by AI technologies; secondly, technology transfer and capacity-building initiatives aimed at empowering Global Majority states; and thirdly, the non-proliferation of dangerous AI capabilities to mitigate potential harm. The document delves into the factors that impede equitable AI governance, establishes the conceptual foundations of benefit-sharing, and outlines the prerequisites for readiness among nations within the Global Majority. It combines analytical and operational perspectives, providing a detailed mapping of specific AI benefit-sharing mechanisms – essentially, a practical toolkit – designed to facilitate the construction of a more just and equitable future in the age of AI. The research acknowledges the urgency of the situation, emphasizing the need for proactive governance and strategic interventions to ensure that the transformative potential of AI is shared broadly and responsibly, rather than concentrated within a select few nations or industries. Ultimately, the paper advocates for a shift in the global AI landscape, prioritizing inclusivity, sustainability, and the well-being of all humanity. It recognizes that simply pursuing technological advancement without considering its equitable distribution is unsustainable and ethically problematic, demanding a deliberate and concerted effort to address the inherent power imbalances within the AI ecosystem.
