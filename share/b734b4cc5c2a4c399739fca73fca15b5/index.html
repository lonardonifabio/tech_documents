AI Literacy : A Briefing

Authors: Sean Musch, Michael Borrelli, Charles Kerrigan, Giannis Tolios, Michael (Yamsi) Tchuindjang, Chakib Achour, Juan Ma Perals EU

This briefing, dated November 2025, explores the rapidly growing importance of AI literacy as a foundational element of digital fluency. It argues that AI literacy is no longer just a niche skill but a critical component of digital capability across all sectors, fundamentally reshaping how individuals and organizations engage with technology. The document highlights the increasing accessibility of AI tools, starting with conversational AI like ChatGPT and expanding to more advanced skills such as Prompt Engineering, Generative AI Tools, and multimodal capabilities including text, code, and visual generation. Specifically, it references rankings of AI skills worldwide, showcasing the global learning landscape and the adoption of tools like Microsoft Copilot, GPT-4, Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, DALL·E, GitHub Copilot, and AI Builder. The briefing emphasizes the shift of AI literacy from purely technical domains into productivity environments and the rise of creative fluency through tools like Stable Diffusion and Midjourney. It underscores the diversification of AI literacy skills, reflecting the expanding applications of AI across coding, business automation, and creative industries. The document implicitly suggests a need for upskilling and development of AI literacy across various industries to adapt to the evolving technological landscape. The core message is that AI literacy is becoming increasingly essential for navigating and utilizing the advancements in artificial intelligence effectively.

Key Concepts:

  • AI literacy is emerging as the foundation of digital fluency, reshaping interactions with technology.
  • AI literacy is becoming a critical component of digital capability across all sectors, focusing on skills like Prompt Engineering and Generative AI Tools.
  • AI literacy is diversifying to include multimodal skills such as text, code, and visual generation, exemplified by tools like Stable Diffusion and Midjourney.
  • As artificial intelligence (AI) becomes a pivotal force in sectors ranging from healthcare to finance, its responsible use is paramount.
  • The AI Literacy framework, outlined in Article 4 of the EU AI Act, represents a crucial step toward ensuring that those involved in deploying and interacting with AI systems are equipped with the knowledge necessary to do so safely and ethically.
  • AI literacy is not merely a compliance exercise but an essential component in ensuring that AI systems are used responsibly and in alignment with human rights, transparency, and accountability.
  • The AI Act does not establish criminal liability or a direct right to compensation.
  • Guidance will be provided by the AI Office through examples, webinars, and Q&As, with potential for national authorities to issue additional enforcement guidance.
  • The Commission intends to publish guidelines addressing requirements under Articles 8 to 15 and Article 25 of the AI Act, covering risk management and human oversight to enhance AI literacy within its workforce.
